# The keys are one table

**The keys are one table** (#80, `ui/keys.go`, the tables in `ui/bindings.go` and `ReadmeKeys` with the README splices in `ui/readme.go` since #275, the epic's "Key legend" rule; #109 made the listing the screen's): `bindings` lists every key play mode accepts, once, as a `binding{key, label, help, keys, screens, modes, global, quiet, when, do}`: the key as the pane draws it (`↑↓`, `[ ]`, `␣`, `pgup pgdn`), a label of one or two lowercase words that is the same everywhere the key appears (`end day`, `post runner`, `send enforcers`, `fund city`, `route dial`, `go to <city>`, filled in by `labelOf`), a help sentence of at most 45 characters (the help modal's column), the raw `tea.KeyMsg` strings it answers to, `screens`, the only thing that lists it (the pane's KEYS shows a binding on exactly the screens it `names`; nil lists it nowhere), `global` for one that works on every screen, listed or not (#109: a key is listed where it is used and works wherever it makes sense, so every global names the screens it belongs on: `b buy`, `s sell`, `x cancel order` the dashboard and the market, `l lie low` the dashboard, `p pay dial` the crew, `d D launder dial` the ledger (`d` up a notch, careful to normal to greedy and round, `D` back one, #473: normal to careful went by way of greedy), `g go to <city>` the dashboard and the map, `r report` the dashboard and the journal, `[ ] city` the market and the map (`[ ] faction` is the rivals screen's own, shadowing it there, #239), `↑↓ pick` the six list screens (`listScreens`: the map and the tree list their own `↑↓←→`; on the rivals screen, where the arrows walk the offers and `[ ]` the faction, it is listed only with two offers or more, `listed: arrowsListed`, #462), and `n end day` and `? help` every screen (`everywhere`), as does `␣ more`, which is listed only under `paneMinWidth` (`listed: stripShown`: a binding's `listed` predicate keeps it out of the pane while it stays live, #420) and works at every width (#111 made it live only there; at 100 columns the pane cuts its lines and the overlay reads them whole), so `keysFor` lists it at 80 columns and not at 100; `c cart` is the dashboard's and the market's own, not global; a screen's own binding for the same key wins there and the global one is not listed: `b` buys a front on the ledger and bails a member on the crew screen (#46), `r` turns a route on the map, `x` and `d` answer a buyer on the market while the cursor is on the buyers, `when`, and `d` there is deliver wherever the cursor is (#460 below); `d` on the map buys the block under the cursor, #194, `docs/property.md`), `quiet` for the frame's keys (`enter`, `1-9`, `tab`, `ctrl+s`, `N`, `q`) that help and the README list and the pane leaves out, and `do`, what it does.
The table's order is the pane's: `n end day`, the cursor keys, the screen's actions, the keys that work everywhere, `? help`.
`keyPlay` dispatches through `lookup` and nothing else: a letter pressed on a screen that does not take it is refused with `pointer`, one sentence per binding that does in the one spelling `<Verb> on the <screen> (<digit>)` (`Hire on the crew screen (4).`), and every pointer in prose is written with `screenPointer(screen)`; the arrows, the paging keys and a key the screen lists but that is not live now are silent, bar one with an `off` refusal (#460 below).
`keysFor(screen)` is what the pane's KEYS renders (`paneKeys`): every live binding that names the screen, the quiet ones left out and none that an earlier binding for the same key shadows there, which is the rule `lookup` dispatches by, so a key is listed exactly where pressing it does what the label says (the status bar lists nothing, #109 above); `modeKeys(mode)` is each modal's footer from the second table, `modeBindings` (`modalFooter` reads it; pages through `modalStep`), `helpLines` is the help modal (the whole table grouped by screen: GLOBAL, every `global` binding once whatever screens list it, then each screen's own, a binding once under the first screen it names, then WORDS; #89 above) and `ReadmeKeys` the README's table, which `go run ./cmd/keys -w` writes between the `<!-- keys:begin -->` / `<!-- keys:end -->` markers.
No other text names a key: not a title, a table cell, a status or a modal body, bar the dashboard's `tutorialLine` (`No sales queued. Press s to sell, n to end the day.`, its two keys in `theme.Key`), the ambitions panel's line on a ready ending (`Ready to take: w on the dashboard walks away on it.`, #498, the walk away's key being the dashboard's and the panel a modal), and the pane's `keyRow`s.
`keys_test.go` pins it: `TestEveryKeyIsInTheTable` (the keys the old switch handled are the table's, no more, and a letter pressed where it does nothing is pointed where it does), `TestLegendMatchesTable` (the pane's KEYS is exactly `keysFor(screen)`, `n end day` first, `? help` last, `q` in neither, and nothing listed that does not `names` the screen), `TestGlobalsAreListedWhereUsed` (each global names the screens above and is taken on every screen), `TestHelpMatchesKeys`, `TestReadmeMatchesKeys` (the README is stale until `cmd/keys -w` runs) and `TestNoKeyHintsOutsideTheLegend` (a grep over every render of the rich fixture at 80x24 and 120x40: no `(4, i)`, `(map, 5)`, `press x`, `(f)` or `a accept · x decline`, and no `(digit)` but the tail of `on the <screen> (<digit>)`).

**Keys act on the shown cursor and wear the region's label** (#239): the map's `r route dial` and `R route target` refuse on a corner (`Pick a route: the dial is on the routes under the grid, not a corner.`) rather than turning the route the hidden routes cursor last sat on, and stay live on the map rather than gated by `mapOnRoutes` because the global `r report` is theirs to shadow there; `←→` on the routes turns the city, as the market's does; the market's `% cut` (`t` until #241) and `o cook` are live and listed on the product table alone (`onProducts`; `o cook` is listed only with a chemist, `listed: hasChemist`, and says to hire one without); a buyer's offer on the market and a faction's on rivals are answered with the same pair, `a accept` / `x decline` (`y` is a confirmation's yes and nothing else); and the market's `x decline` on the buyers shadows the global `x cancel order` there, one key with the region's meaning as the pane's label says, accepted rather than re-lettered.
`TestMapKeysActOnTheShownCursor` pins it.

**A region's key off its region says so** (#460): a binding's `off` is its refusal while the key is the screen's own but not live, and `lookup` says it rather than doing nothing or letting the key fall through to a global that means something else. The market's `d deliver` and `a accept` off the buyers say `Pick a buyer to deliver to: the cursor is on the product table.` (the connects' name where the cursor is there; `Nobody to deliver to in <city>: no buyer is asking.` with none), and `% cut` and `o cook` off the product table `Pick a product to cook: the cursor is on the buyers.` (`offBuyers`, `offProducts`, `marketRegion`): before, `d` off a buyer turned the global launder dial (two blind testers ended on greedy) and `a` and `o` were silent. A binding without `off` keeps the old fall-through. `TestMarketKeysNeverTurnTheLaunderDial` pins it; `d` on the ledger is still the dial. Two more since #469 (`offHouse`, `offAlerts` in `ui/keys.go`, `TestOffKeysNeverFallThrough`): the ledger's `x drop house` off a house row says `Pick a house to drop: x on the ledger drops the house under the cursor.` (`No house to drop: you rent none.` with none), where it fell through to the global `x cancel order` and cancelled an order or a standing order under the market's cursor, which the ledger does not show; and the dashboard's `[ ] alert` with no alert to pick says so (`No alert to pick: ALERTS is empty.`, or that the arrows are on the police panels), where it turned the market's and the map's city unseen. The global `x` still falls through on the screens that have no `x` of their own.

**One digit rule in every picker** (#500): a digit moves the cursor to its row and never acts; `enter` acts. It is `digit(key)` in `ui/format.go` everywhere a list takes digits: `pickerKey` (post, strike, undercut (its notches), assign, guard, driver, captain), the buy picker's kind and offer pages, the propose dialog's kinds and terms, the bribe's two rows, the spy's two lists, the walk-away's ways out, the ambitions, the presets' list, the new run's rows, the move's and the lab's lists, and the buy, sell and route target's product steps and the buy's connect step, which always moved; a dilemma card's `1-3` already only picked (#461). Before, the product and connect pickers moved while the runner, enforcer, front and house pickers acted at once: a playtester moved themself off a corner and bought a front by accident, and `9`, typed for screen 9 over a POST A RUNNER picker, posted runner #9 to Bayport. The footers list `1-9 pick` (`1-2 pick` on the bribe, `1-7 pick` on the new run; the undercut's `1-3 dial` turns its dial as the sale's does); the dialogs' product steps take the digits unlisted, their footers having no room. `TestPickersTakeDigits` walks every picker: it moves the cursor off the first row, `1` puts it back with the mode and the page unchanged, and `enter` acts. The report's `1-3 open` (the lead's lines) is no picker and still jumps.

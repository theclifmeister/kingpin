# The keys are one table

**The keys are one table** (#80, `ui/keys.go`, the epic's "Key legend" rule; #109 made the listing the screen's): `bindings` lists every key play mode accepts, once, as a `binding{key, label, help, keys, screens, modes, global, quiet, when, do}`: the key as the pane draws it (`↑↓`, `[ ]`, `␣`, `pgup pgdn`), a label of one or two lowercase words that is the same everywhere the key appears (`end day`, `post runner`, `send enforcers`, `fund city`, `route dial`, `go to <city>`, filled in by `labelOf`), a help sentence of at most 45 characters (the help modal's column), the raw `tea.KeyMsg` strings it answers to, `screens`, the only thing that lists it (the pane's KEYS shows a binding on exactly the screens it `names`; nil lists it nowhere), `global` for one that works on every screen, listed or not (#109: a key is listed where it is used and works wherever it makes sense, so every global names the screens it belongs on: `b buy`, `s sell`, `x cancel order` the dashboard and the market, `l lie low` the dashboard, `p pay dial` the crew, `d launder dial` the ledger, `g go to <city>` the dashboard and the map, `r report` the dashboard and the journal, `[ ]` the market and the map, `↑↓ pick` the six list screens (`listScreens`: the map and the tree list their own `↑↓←→`), and `n end day` and `? help` every screen (`everywhere`), as does `␣ more`, which is live only under `paneMinWidth` (`when: stripShown`, #111), so `keysFor` lists it at 80 columns and not at 100; `c cart` is the dashboard's and the market's own, not global; a screen's own binding for the same key wins there and the global one is not listed: `b` buys a front on the ledger, `r` turns a route on the map, `x` and `d` answer a buyer on the market while the cursor is on the buyers, `when`), `quiet` for the frame's keys (`enter`, `1-8`, `tab`, `ctrl+s`, `N`, `q`) that help and the README list and the pane leaves out, and `do`, what it does. The table's order is the pane's: `n end day`, the cursor keys, the screen's actions, the keys that work everywhere, `? help`. `keyPlay` dispatches through `lookup` and nothing else: a letter pressed on a screen that does not take it is refused with `pointer`, one sentence per binding that does in the one spelling `<Verb> on the <screen> (<digit>)` (`Hire on the crew screen (4).`), and every pointer in prose is written with `screenPointer(screen)`; the arrows, the paging keys and a key the screen lists but that is not live now are silent. `keysFor(screen)` is what the pane's KEYS renders (`paneKeys`): every live binding that names the screen, the quiet ones left out and none that an earlier binding for the same key shadows there, which is the rule `lookup` dispatches by, so a key is listed exactly where pressing it does what the label says (the status bar lists nothing, #109 above); `modeKeys(mode)` is each modal's footer from the second table, `modeBindings` (`modalFooter` reads it; pages through `modalStep`), `helpLines` is the help modal (the whole table grouped by screen: GLOBAL, every `global` binding once whatever screens list it, then each screen's own, a binding once under the first screen it names, then WORDS; #89 above) and `ReadmeKeys` the README's table, which `go run ./cmd/keys -w` writes between the `<!-- keys:begin -->` / `<!-- keys:end -->` markers. No other text names a key: not a title, a table cell, a status or a modal body, bar the dashboard's `tutorialLine` (`No sales queued. Press s to sell, n to end the day.`, its two keys in `theme.Key`), and the pane's `keyRow`s. `keys_test.go` pins it: `TestEveryKeyIsInTheTable` (the keys the old switch handled are the table's, no more, and a letter pressed where it does nothing is pointed where it does), `TestLegendMatchesTable` (the pane's KEYS is exactly `keysFor(screen)`, `n end day` first, `? help` last, `q` in neither, and nothing listed that does not `names` the screen), `TestGlobalsAreListedWhereUsed` (each global names the screens above and is taken on every screen), `TestHelpMatchesKeys`, `TestReadmeMatchesKeys` (the README is stale until `cmd/keys -w` runs) and `TestNoKeyHintsOutsideTheLegend` (a grep over every render of the rich fixture at 80x24 and 120x40: no `(4, i)`, `(map, 5)`, `press x`, `(f)` or `a accept · x decline`, and no `(digit)` but the tail of `on the <screen> (<digit>)`).

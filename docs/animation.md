# Animation

**Animation** (#152, from the investigation in #150; milestone 8): a **scene** is a short, skippable, deterministic animation drawn through the model's own `View` and driven by a `tea.Tick` that exists **only while a scene is on screen**. The one rule that changed: the UI redraws on a key, a resize, *and* a tick while `Model.scene != nil`; never a tick in play mode, never inside fast-forward's loop, never in the harness (it does not import `ui`), never in the test fixtures or the README's captures (they construct with `Options{Anim: false}`), and `Init` still returns nil (the start menu's loop starts on the first `WindowSizeMsg`, inside `Update`). `internal/ui/anim` is the package: it imports `theme` and nothing else of `ui` (the model imports it), and the effects are ports of TerminalTextEffects read through ttfx, Omarchy's screensaver, with MIT attribution in `internal/ui/anim/NOTICE` (a new effect goes in the NOTICE's list). The API: `anim.Scene` (`Frame(t, w, h) []string`, h lines of at most w cells, and `Done(t)`), both pure functions of t, so a frame asked for twice is the same frame; `anim.Canvas` (cells of a rune and a `lipgloss.Color`, `Lines()` rendering each row as runs of one colour through `theme.Fg`, a cell set outside it dropped, so a scene wider than its room is cut, never wrapped); `anim.Text` (`NewText(art)`, the block art or the words a scene resolves to, `Origin(w, h)` centred, `Cells()` in reading order); `anim.Seed(seed, day, name) *rand.Rand`, a scene's own stream from the run's seed, the day and the scene's name, **never `game.RNGFor`'s** (a scene reads the world and never writes it: `TestSceneNeverTouchesTheWorld` plays a run with animation on and off to day 10 and wants them gob-equal); `anim.Frame` (30 fps); `anim.Decrypt(text, accent, over, rng)`, the first effect (the text typed in as blocks, every cell churning through cipher glyphs in `theme.Dim` and settling left to right, a flash of `theme.Text` as it is discovered, the settled text in the accent; the original's three phases, timed to the length given rather than its frame counts); `anim.Player` (the scene on screen: `Scene`, `Accent`, `Idle`, `Rest`, `Next`; `Tick(now)` advances its clock off the time each tick fired, the first setting the start, so a frame is never read off the wall clock and a test drives it with times of its own; `Frame(w, h)`; `T()`, `Pass()`); and `anim.Scenes()`, the registry of `Named{Name, New(seed)}` that `TestScenesFit` walks (the title for now; #161 grows it). **The model** (`ui/scene.go`): `Model.scene *anim.Player`, `play(p)` puts a scene up (`sceneGen` counts scenes so a tick outstanding when one ended lands on the old generation and is dropped, `ticking` says one is on its way), `stop()` takes it off, `tick()` is the command every branch of `Update` ends with (the next frame's `tea.Tick` while a scene is up and none is on its way, else nil), `onFrame` is a `frameMsg{at, gen}` landing (the clock moves, an interstitial that has run out comes off), and `skip()` at the top of `handleKey` (after `ctrl+c`) ends an interstitial on any key and consumes it (an `Idle` player, the title's, plays on and the key falls through), so the mechanism is there for #154's card before the first interstitial exists. **The title's idle loop** is the proof: `anim.Kingpin` is the block art (six rows, fifty columns, a Go constant), `anim.Title(rng)` decrypts it in `theme.Money` over `anim.TitleLength` (1.5 s), and `titleLoop` (on every `WindowSizeMsg`) runs it behind the start menu's box as an `Idle` player resting `anim.TitleRest` (4 s) then restarting on `anim.Seed(0, pass, "title")`, the way Omarchy's screensaver loops effects over its logo; `viewStart` draws the frame (`titleArt`, at the terminal's width, six rows) above the box, which sits under it; the loop stops when a run starts or continues (`startRun`, `continueRun`, `quit` call `stop`) and under 80x24 or with animation off the menu draws exactly as it did. **The off switch**: `ui.Options{Anim bool}` through `ui.New(cfg, opts)` / `ui.NewSlot(cfg, n, opts)`; `cmd/kingpin -no-anim` and `KINGPIN_NO_ANIM` (any value) turn it off, read where the options are built in `cmd/kingpin`, never in `ui`; every test fixture (`newTestModel`, `richModel`, `richFixture`, `richModelSeeded`, the captures) passes `Anim: false`, and `newAnimModel` in `anim_test.go` is the one fixture that holds a scene. **Every scene**: any key skips it and is consumed; **The effect set** (#153, `anim/effects.go` and one file an effect, each ≤ 300 lines, each credited in `anim/NOTICE` beside the ttfx and TTE files it is read from): the effects a scene draws from, ported as **pure frame functions**, not the engine: no per-character motion objects or waypoints, a function of `(t, w, h, seed, text)` that fills a `Canvas`, every die thrown at construction off the rng the scene passes and `Frame` reading what they decided. One shape for all of them, Decrypt's: `anim.Maker` is `func(text Text, accent lipgloss.Color, over time.Duration, rng *rand.Rand) Scene`, every effect resolves the text in the accent at `over` and holds it after (the frame at `t >= over` is the still text, whatever a short length left unfinished), and `anim.Effects` is the table by name, `Effect{Name, New Maker, Needs, Dice}`, `anim.Names()` its names sorted. **`Needs{Text bool; MinW, MinH int}`** is what an effect needs of a scene, `Needs.Met(text, w, h)` the check, and **`anim.Still(text, accent)`** the fallback where a scene lacks it: the text at once, `Done` from t = 0, so a `Player` given one is over on its first tick. **`anim.Reverse(maker)`** plays an effect from its end (`Frame(over − t)`), so `Reverse(PourFrom(Up))` is the text falling off the bottom of the canvas (#156's cash figures). **The nine that resolve a text** (colour is decoration on a shape whose motion is in the characters): `decrypt` (#152's);

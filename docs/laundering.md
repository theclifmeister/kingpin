# Fronts are the laundering

**Fronts are the laundering.** `World.Fronts` (bought with dirty cash through `BuyFront`, priced by `laundering.Sim.Offers` from `internal/content/laundering.toml`, gated by `unlock_cash` like products) each wash `min(throughput x dial.mul, dirty cash over the float)` from dirty to clean every day, pay `upkeep` in clean cash (unpaid, the front shuts for `upkeep_freeze_days`) and roll an audit at `audit_risk x dial.risk`; an audit freezes the front for `audit_freeze_days`, seizes `audit_seize` of what it washed that day and emits `FrontAudited`. `World.Laundering.Dial` (`careful | normal | greedy`, `events.Launder`) is a persistent setting like the pay dial. The wash never takes dirty cash below `float`, so fronts can never starve the street of restock money (that is what "broke" means). **The laundering sim folds the tree** at the top of its step (#118, `laundering.New` takes it): `wash_mul` on every front's throughput, `audit_risk_mul` on `audit_risk x dial.risk`, `audit_seize_mul` on what an audit takes, `upkeep_mul` on a front's upkeep (`Sim.FrontUpkeep`, the ledger's), `audit_freeze_cut` off `audit_freeze_days` (`Sim.AuditFreezeDays`, never under a day) and `float_mul` on the float; the folded float is **one number on the world**, `World.Float(tree, base)` = `base x FloatMul` (the lowest owned wins), which the wash (`laundering.Sim.Float`, `Washable`) and the road (`logistics.Sim.Budget`) read, each passing its own copy of the tree and the file's float, so the two cannot disagree about where the street's restock money starts and a world owning no float node reads the file exactly (#113's supply contracts fold their own `[supply] float` through the same function: a contract is the street's restock, so the laundering float is what is left *for* it, not a line it keeps); the public reads (`Throughput`, `AuditRisk`, `Capacity`, `Upkeep`, `AnyAuditRisk`) fold for themselves so the ledger prints what the dice use. `TestLaunderingNodesPullTheirWay` (`sim/laundering`) probes every number the branch can move with each node owned alone and checks it moves the ones it names and no other; `TestLaunderingInvariantsUnderTheBranch` (harness) runs the laundered player with the whole branch and pins the pools, the folded capacity, the frozen fronts and that the wash and the road keep to the same halved float. Laundering steps *after* heat, so heat reads yesterday's audit off the front's `Audited`/`AuditDial` record the next morning: `audit_heat` in `heat.toml` every time, plus `audit_evidence` only if the front was being run greedy (#27: a case is built from what you do). Accountants (`[role.accountant]` in `crew.toml`, only in the hiring pool once a front is owned) add `accountant_throughput` to every front and cut audit risk by `accountant_risk_cut`, both scaled by skill; a skimming accountant takes from the previous day's wash, in clean cash (`CrewSkimmed.FromWash`). `NetWorth` counts clean cash, stock at supplier price and fronts at cost.

**Fronts grow by levels** (#192, from #191's report: the boss held $30M clean at day 200 against $15k/day of bills). `Front.Level` and `Front.Invested` (zero is the front as bought, no schema bump; `Front.Grew` the day its growth made the paper) are clean cash put into the place for clean income of its own: `laundering.toml [[front]]` gains `income` (clean a day at level 1), `level_cost` (the first level's price, clean), `level_mul` (each level's cost, income, throughput and upkeep over the last: 1.2) and `max_level` (8), and a `[growth]` table: `audit_level` (0.2, added to the audit-risk multiplier per level), `legit_ratio` (3.0: the wash a front's own income has to explain) and `headline_level` (3: the level whose reaching makes the paper). The numbers: `income` is `level_cost / 100` at every front, so a level pays itself back in 100 days whatever the front or the level (`TestLevelledLaundromatPaysBack`: the laundromat at level 8, $1.65M in, $16.5k/day, 101 days, inside tier 3's 120); the ladder is $260M in all and the cheapest next level is always a few million at most, so the boss's pile drains at the rate it grows ($7.6M clean at day 200, the median of ten seeds, against $30M before; $42M in levels earning $373k/day net, the wash ladder's own scale: `TestBossPileDrains`). The maths (`laundering.go`): `levelMul` is `level_mul^level`, exactly one at level zero; `income(fc, L)` is the sum of `income × level_mul^k` for `k < L`; `levelCost(fc, from, n)` the sum of `level_cost × level_mul^k` for the next `n`; throughput is `(file × levelMul + accountants) × dial × wash_mul`, upkeep `file × levelMul × upkeep_mul`; the public reads (`Income`, `LevelCost`, `MaxLevel`, `Levels` → `game.LevelOffer`, `Sim.Invest`, `LegitIncome`, `Growth`) are what the ledger prints. **Invest** (`World.Invest(LevelOffer)` in `actions.go`, priced by `Sim.Levels` as `BuyFront` is by `Offers`): whole levels at a time, applied at once, clean cash only (`ErrNoCleanCash`, `Fund`'s rule, whose text is now the general one), `ErrFrontMaxed` at the top, `ErrBadQuantity` for none; it stamps `Level`, `Invested`, `Stats.Invested` and `Today.Invested` (`Investment{Front, Levels, Cost}`, `InvestedToday`), and `NetWorth` counts a front at `Cost + Invested`. **The step**: `reportGrowth` first reports today's investments (`FrontInvested`, report-only) and, the first time a front stands at `headline_level` or past it, `FrontGrew` (a `laundering` headline: notoriety through the reputation sim's `headline` source and pressure through the law's, plus `law.toml [pressure] front_grew` = 5 where you are, read off `Front.Grew == t.Day-1` since the law steps before this sim; never evidence, `TestFrontGrowthIsNewsNotEvidence`); then for every open front the wash, the upkeep out of the pile as it stands, and *then* the levels' income (`Stats.Earned`, `CashLaundered.Earned`): a front whose upkeep the pile cannot cover shuts (`upkeep_freeze_days`) and earns nothing that day, so a levelled front pays its own way only out of yesterday's income, kept; spend every clean dollar on levels and a morning with nothing over the float to wash shuts the places that were paying (`TestLevelIncomeLandsAfterUpkeep`; the ruling was income before upkeep, swapped so the reserve means something). **The audit** is the one roll there was (no new dice): `auditRisk` takes the day's wash and multiplies the file's risk by `growthRisk` = `(1 + audit_level × L) × (1 + max(0, wash / (legit_ratio × income) − 1))`, one at level zero, so a run that never invests is byte-for-byte the run before (`TestNoInvestIsTheOldRun`: the laundered player hashed daily on the file and on the file with the levels taken off, 120 days, three seeds) and from the first level the books have to explain the wash: a big washer with a token level is the one the audit finds, a front whose income covers its wash is not (`TestLevelsPullTheirWay`: every level moves income, throughput, upkeep, the next price and the risk by the file's numbers and nothing else; the ledger reads the risk at today's throughput, the sim at what actually washed). `LegitIncome` is every front's income net of its upkeep, open or shut, an unlevelled front at its upkeep alone (a row of fronts that only wash reads as the loss on paper it is): the number #49's `businessman` reads against the street. **The greed curve** is on the levels, not the margin: the boss that levels is audited 52 times over ten seeds against 39 on the file without levels and never fewer on a seed, and carries more notoriety and pressure the morning its growth makes the paper (`TestLevelsDrawTheAuditors`; the boss runs the city at pressure 100 most of the run, so the paper's +5.8 reads only where there is room). Across the margin (`cmd/balance -margin`, `harness.BossAt`, the levels alone: the fronts and the nodes stay at `BossMargin` so the road is not starved) the issue's "audited more, ends lower" is not a property of the sim: a front's own wash lands before its upkeep, so the reserve matters only on a morning with nothing to wash, which the boss never has, and a level's return is bounded by its payback, so a level bought sooner is money; `TestInvestingEverything` pins what holds (margin 1 holds less clean in hand on nine seeds of ten) and logs the rest. `laundered` never invests (tier 3 keeps its number); `boss` invests the cheapest next level of any front it owns, one a day, at `BossMargin` (`harness.Invest`), which moved its tier-4 row from $85.1M to $101.6M and its tier-3 row from $18.03M to $18.69M, both in band.

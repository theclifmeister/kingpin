# All tuning is data

**All tuning is data.** `internal/content/*.toml` is embedded and decoded into `content.Config`; every sim's `New` takes the one `*content.Config` (`sim.Default`, #144) and copies what it reads into unexported fields, so a sim folds its own tuning and never another's by construction: heat copies `Heat`, `Market`, `Routes.Shipping`, `Upgrades`, `Reputation.Effects`, `Crew.Lieutenant`, `Law` and `Houses.Houses`; market `Market`, `City`, `Routes.Shipping`, `Upgrades`, `Reputation.Effects`, `Buyers`, `Suppliers` and `Rivals.Pricewar`; logistics `Routes`, `City`, `Market`, `Upgrades` and the laundering float; laundering `Laundering`, `Crew.Informant` and `Upgrades`; crew `Crew`, `Names.Crew`, `Names.Chemists` (#47), `Reputation.Effects` and `Upgrades`; rivals `Rivals`, `Names.Rivals`, `Reputation.Effects`, `Law.Effects` and `Upgrades`; territory `City`, `Upgrades` and `Houses.Houses`; law `Law`, `Names.Chiefs` and `Names.DAs`; news `Headlines`, `Dilemmas` and `Progression`; reputation `Reputation`. A test that tunes one line copies the config (`forced := *cfg`) and builds the sim off the copy, as `harness.Elect` does. Balance changes should be TOML edits, verified with `cmd/balance` and the harness tests in `internal/harness/balance_test.go`, and `crew_test.go`, which pin the difficulty curve (always-aggressive gets indicted fast, always-quiet survives and earns less, managed beats quiet, crewed beats managed, upgraded beats crewed) and the crew invariants (loyalty monotone in pay, no skim above the threshold). `TestMoneyCurve` pins the economy's scale (issue #24): one row per progression tier giving the band the best policy's median net worth must land in at that tier's checkpoint (`harness.TierDays`, the `checkpoint` of each `[[tier]]` in `progression.toml` since #147, so the harness and the game name the same tiers): tier 1 `managed` ($85k at day 30, band $50k-$200k), tier 2 `crewed` ($605k at day 70, $500k-$2M), tier 3 `boss` ($16.7M at day 120, $5M-$20M) and tier 4 `boss` ($86M at day 200, $50M-$200M; $81M before #192's levels, whose income the boss reinvests), all enforced since #60; the tier-5 row waits for #48. `TestMoneyCeilings` logs, per row, what the policy makes with the rival kept out, with heat off, and with both (`harness.NoRival`, `harness.NoHeat`), so a missed band names its wall: on `main` before #60 the tier-3 and tier-4 rows (`laundered` $1.0M, `delegated` $3.3M) could not reach their bands even with both boxed ($3.9M and $8.3M), which is what said the street itself was tier-1 sized. What #60 changed, and why (the tuning rules of thumb, continued): (1) **the ladder is the tiers**: heroin and meth are what tier 1 and 2 sell (heroin 14 a corner a day at heat 1.8, meth 3 at 2.2; per unit the ladder gets hotter, per dollar cooler, which is what climbing it is for), and designer is the port's product, forty a standard corner at heat 2.5, unlocked at the wholesaler's line and sold by Bayport's supplier only (`city.toml [city.market] designer = { no_supply = true }` at home, `ProductMarket.NoSupply`, `Buy` refuses with `ErrNotSupplied`, the market sim restamps the flag from the config every day so an old save follows the file), so it reaches home by the road and never through a tier-2 crew's supplier. A cash gate cannot do that job: `crewed` never spends, so its peak cash crosses $500k on day 66 and $700k on day 90, while the boss, which buys fronts, the tree and lots, crosses $700k on day 134; any gate that spares the tier-2 crew until day 120 shuts the tier-3 player out until day 130, and the $500k gate the boss clears by day 75 gave `crewed` designer from day 66 and an indictment by day 114. (2) **Heat scales with the operation, never with the lone trader**: the two tier-priced Security nodes `ghosts` ($250k) and `cutouts` ($1.5M) each multiply `crew_heat` by 0.4 (`crew_heat_mul`, a product; `heat.Sim.CrewHeat`), so a runner's unit at tier 4 draws 3% of one you move and the always-aggressive trader, who has no runners, is indicted exactly as fast with the whole branch (`TestSecurityBranchSoftensAggressive` is the guard: a `sale_heat_mul` or `street_units` node strong enough for tier 4 would take that trader under the sting line). (3) **Fronts cover the pile**: the wash ladder tops out near $400k a day and a tier-4 operation makes more, so `dirty_cash_cover` (10x what the fronts cost, on top of `dirty_cash_threshold`) is the pile the police read nothing into; a rich hider with no fronts is covered for nothing and #27 holds (`TestRichHiderIsNeverIndicted`). Without it the boss sat on $17M dirty at day 200 and lay low every other day for it. (4) A lieutenant brings eight people, not four (`[role.lieutenant] crew`), so a boss can work the hub's six corners itself and leave home fully staffed. (5) The rival's pace (below). Tier 1 and 2 moved +4% and -12%; every difficulty and acceptance test held, and the two fixtures that moved were `runCity` (every rung unlocked from day 0, so the temper that skims does not unlock a product a day later) and `TestReputationCannotMaxAllThree` ($1M in the bag instead of $10M: the bigger pile plus an aggressive dump of the new ladder was an arrest on day 3). `TestIdleMarketStaysNearEquilibrium` allows 25% because supply shocks (a slump cuts demand, not price) hold an idle market's mean about a fifth over base in every city. `laundering_test.go` pins the wash invariants (pools never negative, a day's wash never over capacity, frozen fronts wash nothing, greedy washes more and is audited more than careful, an audit is evidence only when greedy, laundered beats crewed). Day counts in the harness (`harness.Horizon`, `TierDays`, `-days`) are where the tooling *looks*, never a run length: the game has no day cap, a run ends only through an ending, and the player sets the pace. Do not add mechanics that end a run merely for playing on (#27 removed the one that did). `territory_test.go` pins the corner invariants: served demand is exactly the worked corners' share, losing every runner loses every corner within `drift_days`, and no corner means nothing sells. Products above the starting three are gated by `unlock_cash` in `market.toml`: the market sim lists a product the first day after peak cash reaches it, in every city, and a city's `no_supply` says its supplier does not sell it there (designer, the port's product). Sale heat is per unit moved (`sale_heat` per `street_units` heat-weighted units), so heat scales with volume, not with how many products exist. **Quality** (#47, `[quality]`, per-product `cut_max`, `cut_cost` and `cook_cost`) is `docs/quality.md`: the price pays the lot's quality, the corners remember it, hard product under the line overdoses, and every connect sells at the default, so the pinned rows stand.

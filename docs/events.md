# Events are the cross-sim channel

**Events are the cross-sim channel.**
Sims never call each other, and no package under `internal/sim/` imports another (`TestSimsNeverImportEachOther` in `sim/sim_test.go` parses every non-test file's imports, #144; a query two sims share, `game.Eligible`, lives in `game`).
A sim emits typed events (`internal/events/events.go`, each with a stable `Kind()`) via `Tick.Emit`; later sims in the same tick read `Tick.Events()` to react (territory, crew and heat consume the market's `PlayerSold`, the crew sim for a lieutenant's cut and the heat sim for their temper; crew applies the rival sim's `CornerStruck` toll to enforcers by nerve; heat adds the heat carried on `CornerStruck`, `RivalTippedPolice`, a crackdown `WarEscalated` and the logistics sim's `ShipmentSeized`; the law sim counts the violence, the hard product on `PlayerSold` and an `Enforcement` that shows the chief's hand, and honours the world sim's `Incident` the same tick when it carries a new chief or a snap election (#44, `docs/incidents.md`); news consumes everything).
After the tick, the bus delivers events to the UI (`Model.onEvent`) for the alerts and the journal.
Events are not saved: a sim that must act on last night's event next step (the rivals sim on a defector) reads a queue the emitting sim keeps on its own state (`Crew.Leads`, #144), never another sim's.
Every event that names the rival by name carries a `Faction` id beside it, and since #43 the news sim fills `{{.Leader}}` and `{{.Faction}}` from the event's own faction (`crew(d, ev.Rival)`), so a line about the second faction names the second faction (`docs/rival.md`).
The table's events (#43): `FactionPushed` (two factions over a corner; the heat sim adds its `Heat` in its `City`, the law `[pressure] factions`), `RivalAbsorbed` (`RivalAbsorbed` and `RivalScattered` templates), `RivalLeaderArrested` (`RivalLeaderArrested` and `RivalLeaderKilled`), `CrewPoached` (a headline when they went, report-only when they stayed; the crew sim reads it), and `TrustSpread` (report-only).
Every template can name the paper's regulars beside the event's own slots (#44: `{{.DA}}`, `{{.Chief}}`, `{{.Leader}}`, `{{.Faction}}` are filled on every line from the world, `{{.Route}}` and `{{.Days}}` from an incident), and an `Incident`'s key is `Incident` plus its id in camel case.
Adding an event kind means adding it to `events.All` (`internal/events/all.go`, every kind's zero value) and then either a headline template in `internal/content/headlines.toml` or a line in the news test's `reportOnly` set (#274).
Two tests hold that: `TestAllListsEveryKind` (`events/events_test.go`) parses the package's source with `go/parser`, finds every type with a `Kind` method and fails on one missing from `All` or listed twice, and on a `Kind` that is not the one line returning the type's own name; `TestEveryEmittedEventHasTemplate` (`sim/news/news_test.go`) walks `All` and fails on a kind with neither a template nor a `reportOnly` line, on one with both, on a `reportOnly` entry that is no kind, and on a template in `headlines.toml` that no kind writes.
A kind writes the template of its own name unless the test's `templateKeys` lists the keys it picks among (`PriceShock` as `PriceShock`, `PriceShockSeized` or `PriceSlump`; `Unlocked` by its gate; `Enforcement` by the rungs in `heat.toml`; `Incident` by the rows in `incidents.toml`; `ReputationShifted` by axis and direction; and so on); a kind that writes a new variant key adds it there.
`reportOnly` is report-only bookkeeping like `PriceMove`, `CrewPaid`, `RivalUndercut`, `WholesaleBought`, `SupplyBought`, `SupplyShort`, `StandingShort`, `SupplierBought`, `CreditTaken`, `DebtPaid`, `ShipmentSent`, `ShipmentArrived`, `LieutenantActed`, `CityFunded`, `PlayerUndercut`, `StockMoved`, `RentPaid`, `HouseCompromised`, `RivalScouted`, `PoliceTipped`, `StockCut`, `CookOrdered` or `Cooked` (#47), `TrustSpread` (#43), `AssetFrozen` (#48), `AssetUpkeepPaid` (#351), the deliberately silent `CrewTurnedInformant` and `LieutenantFlipped`, the card's `DilemmaDrawn` and `DilemmaAnswered`, and the loop's own `DayEnded`, `Headline` and `GameOver` (found unlisted when the set was made explicit, #274). #48's `AssetBought`, `TaskForceFormed`, `AssetSeized`, `TunnelFound` and `UnlockedAsset` pick their template off `Tick.Sub("assets:news")`; the task force's rung is `EnforcementTaskforce`.

**Intel's events** (#45): `IntelGained` (report-only: a fact filed tonight, emitted by whichever sim wrote it; the report's INTEL section), `SpyPlanted`, `SpyFound` (`SpyFound` made and home, `SpyShot` dead; `Why: "gone"` report-only) and `IntelFalse` (`IntelFalseRoute`, `IntelFalseStash`: a lie that bit, naming the faction).
The news picks their templates off `Tick.Sub("intel:news")`.
`RivalScouted` carries the numbers read (`Cash`, `Income`, `Muscle`, `Wages`) since the snapshot moved into the file.

**How the news sim writes an event** (#275): `news.Sim.Step` makes one `reporter` a tick (`sim/news/reporter.go`: the report, the headlines, the default `data` a line names, and the money the day's events moved, booked by category and pile into the night's cash flow, whose opening is CASH BEFORE, #351, `docs/market-and-journal.md`) and hands it every event in the order they were emitted.
When the report and the cash flow are written, `Sim.lead` ranks the night and writes the three biggest changes as `DayReport.Lead` and into the journal under `digest` (#354, `sim/news/digest.go`, `docs/market-and-journal.md`). It reads the tick's events, the world and `World.Flows`, and draws no dice.
`reporter.report` passes each one to the method of the sim that emits it, in the day loop's order, and the first to take it is the only one that would: `reportMarket`, `reportLogistics`, `reportTerritory`, `reportRivals`, `reportCrew`, `reportHeat`, `reportLaw`, `reportLaundering`, `reportReputation`, one file each (`report_<sim>.go`).
An event two sims emit is written where the earlier of them steps.
A new event kind's line goes in its emitter's file.
The order the templates draw off the streams is the order the events were emitted, as before the split, so the split moved no number: `TestSeedDigest` and every pinned run are unchanged, and the journal and the reports hash the same over 200 days.


**The dials** (#275) live beside the events in `internal/events/dials.go`: the sell dial `Dial` (quiet, normal, aggressive), `Pay` (stingy, fair, generous), `Force` (warn, push, hit), `Launder` (careful, normal, greedy) and `Ship` (slow, normal, fast) are three-way, and `RouteDial` (off, slow, normal, fast, #61) the one four-way.
Each is an int in that order with one names table that drives its `String`, the notches the UI draws (`DialNames`, `PayNames`, `ForceNames`, `LaunderNames` for `dialRow`, `payRow`, `launderRow`, `cmd/balance`'s `-force` and `-undercut`) and, for the two a config file names, its parse (`ParseDial` for `crew.toml`'s `[lieutenant.personality.X] dial`, read by `LieutenantPersonality.SellDial` and refused at load by `CrewConfig.validate` with the names it could have been; `ParseForce` for `rivals.toml [war] dial`, `WarTuning.Force`, where a word it does not know is the war boxed, `harness.NoWar`).
A value off the table reads as the old switches' default, the middle notch of a three-way dial and off for a route (`TestDialsReadTheirTable`).
The config rows are picked by one helper, `content.threeWay` (the low row at 0, the high at 2, the middle otherwise), behind `DialTable.For` (the market's `Sim.Dial` and the heat sim's `dialHeat` and `dialFill`), `RoutesConfig.DialFor`, `LaunderingConfig.DialFor` and `CrewConfig.PayFor`.
**No dial implements `encoding.TextUnmarshaler`** (or `TextMarshaler`): every one is saved as a JSON int on the `World` (`Crew.Pay`, `Laundering.Dial`, a shipment's and a route's dial, an order's), and `encoding/json` refuses a number into a type with `UnmarshalText`, so it would make every save unreadable; the TOML fields that name a dial therefore stay strings and read through the parse.

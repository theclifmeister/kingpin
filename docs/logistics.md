# Logistics is the road, and the road is a dial

**Logistics is the road, and the road is a dial** (#30, #61, `internal/sim/logistics`, `World.Routes`, `World.Shipments`, `World.Logistics`, tuning in `routes.toml`). `[[route]]` edges join two cities with a `mode` (car, truck, boat), `days` in transit, `capacity` per shipment, `cost` per unit (dirty) and `risk` of interception per day in transit, and run from `from` to `to`: every route in the file runs from Bayport, the wholesale city, into Eastside. Shipping is not an action. Each route has a **setting** on the world, `World.Routes[id]` = `RouteSetting{Dial events.RouteDial, Target map[product]int, Days map[product]int}` (`w.Route(id)`; `HasTargets()`), set by `SetRoute(id, dial)`, `SetRouteTarget(id, product, units)` and `SetRouteDays(id, product, days)` in `actions.go` and persistent like the pay dial (a product keeps units or days of demand, never both: setting one clears the other, #115, and a map left empty is nil again, so a cleared route is the zero value and a pre-#115 save loads with `Days` nil, no schema bump): the dial is `off | slow | normal | fast` (`events.RouteDial`; off is the zero value, so a schema-9 save loads with every route off and needs no bump; `RouteDial.Ship()` is the `events.Ship` the shipment rides at, whose `[dial.*]` multiplies the days, never under one, and the risk on each, so `logistics.Sim.Days`, `DayRisk` and `Risk` are what the dice use and what the map shows (#42: a bought checkpoint or customs agent on the edge, `RouteSetting.Bought` live by `World.CheckpointLive`, takes law.toml's `checkpoint_cut` / `customs_cut` off the day's risk through `Sim.Cut`, and the sim clears a deal the cold has ended the tick after; `docs/law.md`); they take the world since #119, because the tree folds into them: `route_days_mul` on the days, still never under one, `route_risk_mul` on the risk of every day, `route_capacity_mul` on `Sim.Capacity`, `fare_mul` on `Sim.Fare`, a price per unit, so a dollar fare halves to fifty cents and a shipment's `Cost` is the fare over its units rounded up to the dollar, and while `wholesale_mul` is the market sim's fold onto the wholesale connect's price (`market.Sim.SupplierRatio(w, sup)`, #72); `Sim.Effects(w)` is the fold, made once at the top of `Step` and again for every number the map, the pane and the target dialog show, so the odds the player reads are the ones the dice use), and the target is the stock the route keeps its destination at per product, as units, or as **days of demand** (#115): `logistics.Sim.Target(w, r, product)` is the units a target means this morning, the units as set or `days x World.Demand(to, product)` rounded up (`Sim.DaysTarget`, the same number the market screen shows as `demand/day`, which drifts with the street's price, so the units a days target means drift too; no dice), and `Shortfall` reads it, so holding more corners at the far end raises what the route sends with no change to the setting (`TestRouteDaysTargetFollowsDemand` in the harness, `TestDaysTargetIsDemand` in the sim). The target dialog (`ui/routes.go`, `modeTarget`) is three steps, product, then `units` / `days` on `←→` (the kind opens on what the product keeps), then the number (`numberField`, #112: `max` is the far end's `World.Capacity`, or for days the days of demand that holds), and on days says what they mean today (`Today  3d ≈ 180 units: ~60/day on your corners in Eastside`); the route's pane row, the target dialog's table and the ledger's LOGISTICS column read `target  3d (≈180) Weed · 400 Coke` (`targetLine`), and the LOGISTICS table drops `lost` and then `mode` (the pane carries both) where MAIN is too narrow for the targets. The harness's `distributor` still sets units by hand (`int(DistributorDays x demand)`, truncated, and a target of 0 for a product the lot does not undercut): a days target rounds up, so moving it would move every pinned distributor number. Every day, after the market has sold and before heat, the sim `move`s the road (a shipment on a route an incident has shut, `RouteSetting.ClosedUntil` over the tick's day, `rs.Closed(t.Day)`, #44, sits where it is with `Arrives` one later and no roll, so a shut route moves nothing and nothing on it is seized; otherwise it rolls interception on every shipment in transit off the `logistics` side stream, lands what is due, `ShipmentArrived`, or loses it, `ShipmentSeized`, keeping `Logistics.Seizures` for the market and `Logistics.Lost[route]` for the ledger) and then `run`s the routes with no dice at all: for each route with its dial on and not shut (a shut route refuses new shipments until it reopens; `World.RouteClosed(id)` is tonight's answer for the routes screen, whose pane reads `closed  N nights to go`), in file order, and each product in ladder order, the **shortfall** (`Sim.Shortfall`: target less the destination's stash less what is already `Bound` there) up to `capacity` goes today, out of the source stash first, then whole lots bought from the wholesale connect at the source (`World.Restock`, the sim's, never the player's: the connect flagged `wholesale` in `suppliers.toml` (#72, the Dutchman in Bayport) sells `lot`-unit lots at their price, which the market sim stamps each morning from their `ratio` band (0.385 of street in the neutral band, 0.7 of the street connect's 0.55, the `ticket`'s `wholesale_mul` folded in) once peak cash reaches their `unlock_cash`, into the stash regardless of its capacity, the odd lot's remainder waiting for tomorrow; refused while they are `Frozen` and never past what their day has left (`Supplier.Left`), a shortfall the route sends again tomorrow; `WholesaleBought` names the connect), then the fare; the road spends only what is over the **laundering float** (`Sim.Budget`, the same `float` the wash leaves in the till, passed to `logistics.New` and folded by the tree through `World.Float`, #118; the road never starves the street), lots before fares and never a lot it cannot then send. `World.Send` puts the shipment on the road (`Sent` the tick's day, `Arrives` the dial's days later, the fare off dirty cash, the units off the source stash, `Stats.Shipments`/`Shipped`), the sim emits `ShipmentSent` the same morning, and `Logistics.Days` keeps one `RouteDay` per route per day it spent anything (kept `record_days`, summed by `RouteSpend` for the ledger's week). A run with every route off draws nothing from the side stream and replays byte-for-byte as before the dial. Stock is conserved across a shipment and a seized one never arrives (`TestStockIsConservedAcrossShipments`, whose closure case shuts the route for three ticks after a shipment left and reads it landing three days late with its days intact, and a target raised while it was shut sending the first morning it reopens); fast is seized more than slow (`TestFastIsSeizedMoreThanSlow`); the far city is kept at the target within `capacity` a day, nothing moves under the float and nothing with the dial off (`TestRouteDialKeepsTheTarget`, `TestRunKeepsTheTarget`); a seized shipment is a shortfall the route sends again tomorrow. A seizure is a market shock, not a bust (`TestSeizureIsShockNotEvidence`): the heat sim adds `seizure_heat` in both cities on the route and `seizure_evidence` only if the shipment was sent fast (#27: a case is built from what you did), and the market, which steps before logistics, reads the seizure off `Logistics.Seizures` the next morning and spikes the product in the city it was bound for by `shock_factor` for `shock_days` (`PriceShock.Seized`, headline `PriceShockSeized`); the report's SHIPMENTS block reads as a supply line (bought, left, landed, seized with the dial it was sent at, and each route's cost that day) and says, the first time, that a seizure is the cue to turn the dial down. `World.TotalStock()` counts the road, so wages never end a run while product is in transit; `NetWorth` values it at the destination's supplier price. Bayport's price multipliers and the wholesale connect's lot are where the tier-4 margin comes from; `distributor` is the policy that runs the dial and `TestDistributorBeatsLaundered` pins that it pays. The Phase 4 hooks act on the route setting: corruption (#42) cuts a route's risk, the port and airstrip (#48) add capacity and routes, the driver (#46) is assigned to a route, an incident (#44) closes one (`docs/incidents.md`).

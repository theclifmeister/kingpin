# Upgrades are multipliers the sims read

**Upgrades are multipliers the sims read.** `internal/content/upgrades.toml` is the tree: `[[upgrade]]` nodes with a branch (`content.Branches`: `operations`, `security`, `legal`, `crew`, `laundering`, `logistics`, `street`; fifty-six nodes since #118 and #119, eleven, eleven, seven, ten, six, six and five), a cost (dirty unless `clean = true`; the `retainer`, the `auditlawyer`, the `judge` and, since #118, the `offshore` and `books2` are the five clean-cash nodes, so they are only buyable once the fronts have washed something), `requires` and an `[upgrade.effects]` table of named multipliers and deltas; the file header fixes how each name combines across owned nodes (four rules: product, sum, lowest wins, highest wins) and decode refuses an unknown effect name or a prerequisite not defined earlier. **The vocabulary** (#117, `content.UpgradeEffects` and `game.Effects`, one field a name, by the sim that reads it; `TestFoldEffects` tables every name's rule with two owned nodes and fails on a field the table lacks): the market's `supplier_mul` (lowest), `buy_pressure_mul`, `fill_mul`, `sale_impact_mul`, `demand_mul` (products), `glut_decay_mul` (highest), `buyer_gap_mul` (lowest), `contract_premium_bonus` (sum); heat's `sale_heat_mul`, `crew_heat_mul`, `sting_stock_mul`, `raid_loss_mul` (products), `patrol_cap`, `lie_low_multiplier`, `decay`, `dirty_cash_threshold_mul`, `evidence_arrest` (highest), `cooldown_bonus`, `evidence_cut`, `audit_evidence_cut`, `fall_guys` (sums), `evidence_decay_days` (lowest); the crew's `wage_mul`, `hire_fee_mul` (lowest), `loyalty_loss_mul`, `danger_loyalty_mul`, `skim_chance_mul`, `informant_chance_mul` (products), `crew_slots`, `candidates_bonus`, `pool_days_cut`, `skill_bonus`, `start_loyalty_bonus` (sums); laundering's `wash_mul`, `audit_risk_mul` (products), `audit_seize_mul`, `upkeep_mul`, `float_mul` (lowest), `audit_freeze_cut` (sum); logistics' `route_risk_mul`, `route_capacity_mul` (products), `route_days_mul`, `fare_mul`, `wholesale_mul` (lowest); the street's `robbery_mul`, `rival_push_mul` (products), `drift_days_bonus`, `guard_bonus` (sums). The crew and laundering names are read since #118 (the crew sim: `wage_mul` in `WageAt`, so the roster's column and the bill agree; `loyalty_loss_mul` in `LoyaltyLoss`, which scales every negative day the way respect does; `danger_loyalty_mul` on `danger_loyalty`; `skim_chance_mul` and `informant_chance_mul` on the rolls under the lines, the draws made either way; `crew_slots` in `MaxCrew`, which the UI, `Hire`'s callers and the harness's `staff` take the cap from; `candidates_bonus` in `Candidates` and `pool_days_cut` in `PoolDays`, never under a day, the crew screen's `new faces in Nd`; `skill_bonus` and `start_loyalty_bonus` on a generated candidate, never over 100, and `hire_fee_mul` in `HireFee`, all three fixed at generation like the fee, none adding a draw, so the first faces of a bigger pool are the ones a smaller one drew; `TestCrewNodesPullTheirWay` in `sim/crew` probes every number with each node alone, counting the skims and the turns over a hundred nights on one seed, and `TestCrewInvariantsUnderTheBranch` in the harness holds loyalty monotone in pay, no skim over the line and the roster at the folded cap with the whole branch owned; the laundering sim's reads are in its own paragraph), and the logistics and street names since #119 (the logistics sim's `Days`, `DayRisk`, `Risk`, `Capacity`, `Fare` and `Wholesale`, the territory sim's `DriftDays` and `RobberyChance`, the rivals sim's `Guard` and `PushPace`, each folding through its own `Effects(w)`); `carry_bonus` (sum) is the one delta applied on purchase. `World.Upgrades` is the owned set; `World.BuyUpgrade(tree, id)` (`game/upgrades.go`) checks existence, ownership, prerequisites and the right cash pool, applies at once and is not per-day scratch (only `UpgradesToday`, which the market sim reports as `UpgradeBought`, is). `game.FoldEffects(w, tree)` folds the owned set into a plain `game.Effects` (`identity` is the fold of nothing owned: every multiplier 1, the rest 0); the market and heat sims call it at the top of `Step` and multiply their own tuning by it (supplier price, buy pressure, fill, a sale's price impact, what the worked corners serve — `market.Sim.Demand` is `World.Demand` times `demand_mul`, what an order is served against and what its impact is measured against, while `World.Demand` itself, which the dashboard and the map show, stays the corners' — the glut's decay, the buyers' gaps (`market.Sim.Gaps`, the roll made either way so the side stream never moves) and a contract's premium; sale heat, a runner's heat, decay, lie-low, patrol cap, response cooldown, sting and raid losses, the cash pile's line (`heat.Sim.DirtyCashThreshold`, which the dashboard's and the ledger's warning read so the line shown is the line the dice use), evidence per bust and per greedy audit, `evidence_arrest`), so no sim reads another. The one delta to player state, `carry_bonus`, lands on `Player.CarryLimit` at purchase. The Legal branch lives in the heat sim: the lawyer and the paper trail thin what a bust adds, the accountant's lawyer what a greedy audit files, the retainer lets a point of evidence go cold every `evidence_decay_days` without a new one (the judge sooner; `Heat.EvidenceDay`; #27's rule still holds, a quiet-day sting adds nothing and does not reset the clock), and the fall guys take an indictment or arrest each (`fall_guys` is a count, `World.FallsTaken` what they have taken, `World.FallGuyLeft`, `FallGuyBurned`; `SchemaVersion` 10 turned the pre-#117 `FallGuyUsed` flag into the count, `game.MigrateFallGuys` reading it off the stream a second time as the cities were, `TestSaveMigratesTheFallGuy`). Tuning rules of thumb: heat multipliers cannot make an always-aggressive player last longer, because heat saturates and the DA's file then grows at the response cooldown, so the Security branch's survival lever is `cooldown_bonus`; make it strong enough to keep heat under the sting threshold and aggressive play becomes safe, which is worse. `TestSecurityBranchSoftensAggressive` and `TestUpgradedBeatsCrewed` pin both ends. A node that has to carry a tier's *volume* therefore cuts what a runner's unit draws (`crew_heat_mul`: `ghosts`, `cutouts`), never what yours does: the lone trader stays as hot as ever and only an operation with a crew grows quieter (#60); no node past those adds `sale_heat_mul`, `crew_heat_mul` or `street_units`. **The tree is measured on the operation it is for** (#117): `harness.Upgraded` is `crewed` plus the tree at 3x the price in hand, and `TestUpgradedBeatsCrewed` is the guard that spending on it pays, because a lone trader who buys every node pays for insurance and for crew, front and road nodes it cannot use (measured node by node against the old tree: only `scales` and `regulars` paid the lone trader back, `paper`, `counter` and `stash3` each cost it near their price), so buying everything is a trap for one by design and the pre-#117 guard against `managed` could not survive a tree with more than a dozen nodes. `TestUpgradedOwns` logs what it owns at days 70, 120 and 200 (a median of 29, 34 and 38 of 56 at 3x since #118 and #119: the clean-cash nodes, the tier-3 and tier-4 nodes and, with no front, the Laundering branch and, with no route on, the Logistics branch are what wait), and `TestUpgradedBeatsCrewed` logs the two medians ($1.51M against $1.27M on the whole tree; the Crew branch's `room` and `room2` are worth more corners to the scripted crewed player by day 120 and fewer by day 200, when the rival has taken the extra ground back and the churn of hiring for it is paid for, which is the policy's bluntness, not the node's, since the boss gains on both). **The Logistics and Street branches** (#119, six and five nodes) are the road's and the corners': `tyres` and `compartments` cut every route's risk (a product, 0.56 with both), `trucks` carry half as much again, `drivers` cut the days (never under one), `ticket` (over `supplier2`) takes a tenth off the wholesaler's lot and `forwarder` halves the fare; `boys` hold an idle corner two days longer, `watch` and `dogs` cut the robbery chance (0.42 with both), `frontline` is a body on every held corner in the rival's push odds and `ground` cuts its push chance. The branch slows the rival's taking of ground and never stops it: `TestStreetBranchSlowsTheRivalNeverStopsIt` runs the passive player with all five nodes against an expansionist and pins that every seed still loses corners by the tier-3 checkpoint, no more of them than bare and no sooner over the seeds (the first loss is within 60 days on four seeds of five and day 66 on seed 5, against day 34 bare; holding every seed to 60 days would need `rival_push_mul` at 0.95, a $400k node that does nothing). `TestLogisticsBranchMovesMoreForLess` pins that the distributor's road with the six nodes pays less a unit, is seized less over five seeds and pays at the horizon (median peak $23M to $45M). Neither branch pays the crewed player back in full (`upgraded`, which never runs the road and holds its corners with enforcers, buys Street and not Logistics; `frontline` and `ground` are insurance for a player with an enforcer on every contested corner), and the boss at tier 3 is lower at day 120 for having bought them at `BossMargin` and level at day 200. One table test per sim pins that each node moves the number it names and nothing else (`TestLogisticsNodesMoveTheirNumbers`, `TestStreetNodesMoveTheirNumbers`, `TestRivalNodesMoveTheirNumbers`). The upgrades screen shows one branch at a time under branch tabs (#120; the paragraph on crew and upgrades in the grammar has it), because the tree outgrew 80x24 as columns.

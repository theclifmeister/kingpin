# The harness and cmd/balance

`cmd/balance` policies: `idle | hide | quiet | normal | aggressive | careful | managed | upgraded | crewed | territory | war | diplomat | laundered | funded | distributor | delegated | dealer | stocked | routine | leveraged | boss | pricewar | saboteur | tipster`. An unknown policy silently falls back to `normal`. `-lielow N` overrides the lie-low threshold of the managed-style policies; `-corners N` caps how many corners `territory` and `war` work (`crewed` works as many as it can staff); `-force warn|push|hit` is how hard `war` sends the enforcers; `-rival P` forces the rival's personality; `-cash N` starts every run with that much dirty cash (`-policy hide -cash 5000000` is the rich player who sits on the pile); `-own a,b` starts every run owning those upgrades for free, prerequisites first (`harness.Own`). `upgraded` is `crewed` plus the tree (#117: the cheapest node it can buy at 3x the price in hand, one a day, walking the branches in `harness.TreeOrder`: Security, Operations, Legal, Crew, then Laundering, which waits for a front the way an accountant does, since a bookkeeper with nothing to wash cost the crewed player $300k for nothing, Street and, once a route is on, Logistics, since #119's road nodes do nothing for a player who never runs the road and `crewed` plus the tree never buys them; `harness.BuyUpgrades`, which `distributor` and, at `BossMargin`, `boss` share). `laundered` is `crewed` plus fronts: it buys the cheapest front it lacks when dirty cash is 3x the price, runs the launder dial at normal and drops to careful for 30 days after an audit. `distributor` turns the biggest route into home to normal on day one and keeps its target at `harness.DistributorDays` of home's demand for every product a lot undercuts home's street by `DistributorMargin` (the logistics sim buys the lots and sends them, #61); it is `laundered` until the wholesaler deals with it, then moves to Bayport for good, keeps runners on the corners at home and the rest on the corners there, restocks the corners there at retail, sells everything at home and there, and buys the upgrade tree as `upgraded` does. `-cards decline|first` deals the dilemma cards and answers every one with its last or first choice; without it the harness plays with the deck in the box. `-incidents on|off` (#44) deals the world's incident table or boxes it: on by default, so the tool plays the game as played and prints `incidents: N per run, M of 11 in the table seen: …` (and `-trace` a `day N incident …` line for each), off for a pinned number, since every harness test runs with it boxed (`harness.Run`, `RunFrom` and `RunWith` box it as they box the deck; `harness.Play(cfg, w, days, policy, Options{Cards, Incidents})` is the general form the tool and `TestEveryIncidentFires` use). `diplomat` is `territory` that proposes a truce whenever the rival has taken a corner off it in the last `harness.DiplomatDays` and pays tribute once the truce has been refused twice; it stays at home, where the rival is (`distributor` leaves for the hub). `delegated` is `distributor` that hires the first lieutenant looking for work (firing its least skilled runner to make room), gives them home, keeps `harness.HubCorners` runners on the hub's corners and leaves the rest idle for the lieutenant to post, fills the roster their people make room for, pays them off when their loyalty nears the flip line, and never posts or sells at home itself (the route feeds home either way); `-lt violent|greedy|careful|steady` forces the temper of the lieutenant it hires (by default they come as generated). `funded` is `laundered` that gives the city it is in a quarter of its clean cash whenever the pressure there is over `harness.FundPressure` (60), up to what fills goodwill, and puts the same quarter behind the reform ticket in every city the day its campaign opens (#193, `harness.backReform`, up to what buys the whole swing; `boss` does both, paying every city); the `campaigns:` line prints campaigns backed, won and the clean cash behind them per run; `-chief corrupt|zealous|lazy` and `-da law_and_order|moderate|reform` force the law for the whole run (`harness.Appoint`: the actor is renamed and its clock stopped, so no election or term end replaces it), and the end medians print pressure and goodwill. `boss` (#60) is `delegated` that plays the whole game: it works every corner in the hub, keeps `Territory`'s share of the roster as enforcers and sends them in at push only when the odds the picker shows clear `harness.BossOdds` (0.5; at push that is rare, and a hit at the same line wins corners and loses the run), otherwise talks the way `diplomat` does; sells and routes only the products worth their heat (`harness.BossWorth`: price per point of heat at least a fifth of the best on offer, so the street half of the ladder is dropped once designer is), keeps the route's target at the road's days of demand plus two, buys fronts and the tree at `harness.BossMargin` (1.5x the price in hand), pays generous, and fires a lieutenant the morning their standing orders show the aggressive dial. `-undercut quiet|normal|aggressive` is the dial `pricewar` undercuts at (#68: `territory` that undercuts the biggest rival corner next door every day it can instead of striking; every run prints the undercuts, the units they moved, the corners the rival gave up and its muscle at the end); `leveraged` (#72) is `crewed` that takes whatever it restocks from the cheapest connect where it stands on their book first, up to what they will run it, and pays cash for the rest (the debt is collected on its day by the market sim), `-credit off` withdraws every connect's credit (`harness.NoCredit`; leveraged is then crewed exactly), and the output's `suppliers:` line prints the median relationship with the street connect at the end, the days in debt, the late payments, the freezes, the collections and the credit taken per run. `stashed` (#73) is `laundered` that keeps its stock in stash houses: the cheapest house on offer where it stands once dirty cash is `harness.HouseMargin` (3x) the price and clean cash covers `RentCover` (30) days of the rent, the next (up to `-houses N`, default `harness.StashHouses`, 3; `-houses 1` keeps everything in one) once one there is over `HouseFull` (0.8) of its capacity, moving stock between them after the day's buying so none holds more than `Spread` (0.6) of the city's, and `-fronts off` makes it `crewed` with the houses, so nothing pays the rent; the output's `houses:` line prints the houses held and lost, the rent and the units lost out of them per run. `-rival none` keeps the rival out of a run (`harness.NoRival`), `-heat off` switches heat off (`harness.NoHeat`: no source adds any and the ladder is out of reach), `-pace off` gives the rival its flat pre-#60 pace (`harness.FlatPace`), and every run prints the rival's median corners at `harness.PaceDays` (30, 60, 120) so the pace is a number in the output, and under it the rival's books at the same days (#139, `harness.RivalBooks`: `rival books: day 30 cash N income N/day wages N/day …`, medians of the chest, the take and the wage bill, so whether money can hurt it is a number too); the three boxes are how the ceilings a tier plays against are measured (`TestMoneyCeilings` logs them per row). `saboteur` (#70) is `crewed` that works the rival's books instead of striking: it scouts whenever the snapshot is stale or unread, boosts the rival's biggest corner at the cheapest force whose odds clear `harness.SaboteurOdds` (0.5) while the war is under `SaboteurWar` (0.75) of the crackdown line (warn only under a deal), buys off a head at `SaboteurMargin` (5x) the price while the books as last read show a chest under `SaboteurThin` (3) days of its wage bill, and tips the police on that corner on the nights the rival holds more corners than it, no deal holds and the police are ready to act, keeping the tips up until the raid lands; `tipster` is `saboteur` that tips every night the rival holds a corner, the price of talking to police measured. The output's `books:` line prints the scouts and the reads, the boosts landed and the cash they took, the tips and the raids they brought, the heads bought off, and the rival's muscle, its heat and your file at the end.

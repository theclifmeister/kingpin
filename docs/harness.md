# The harness and cmd/balance

`cmd/balance` takes `-policy P`, `-runs`, `-days`, `-seed`, `-trace` and the flags `-lielow -corners -force -rival -factions -cash -own -cards -incidents -lt -chief -da -undercut -credit -houses -fronts -heat -pace -cut -margin -life -deeds -roles -character -hardda -snitch` (`go run ./cmd/balance -help`); `-runs` under one is refused (#272).
`cmd/balance` policies: `idle | hide | quiet | normal | aggressive | careful | managed | upgraded | crewed | vigilant | territory | war | warlord | diplomat | laundered | funded | corrupt | favoured | distributor | driven | delegated | dealer | stocked | routine | leveraged | boss | pricewar | stashed | saboteur | tipster | cook | retiree | cartel | reckless | informed`.
The list is `harness.Policies` (#274), the registry the tool looks a policy up in: each name with its constructor taking `harness.PolicyOpts` (the lie-low override, `-corners`, `-force`, `-undercut`, `-lt`, `-margin`, `-houses`, `-fronts`; `DefaultPolicyOpts` is the flags unset), in the order `-help` prints, which is generated from it.
An unknown policy exits 2 with the valid names (until #274 it fell back to `normal` and printed normal's numbers under the typo), and so does an unknown value of any string flag with a vocabulary (`-force -undercut -heat -pace -credit -fronts -life -deeds -roles -incidents -cards -rival -lt -chief -da`, `oneOf` in `cmd/balance`; `-rival`, `-lt`, `-chief` and `-da` take `content.Personalities`, `LieutenantPersonalities`, `ChiefPersonalities` and `DAStances`).
The summary's counters live in one struct a subsystem (`cmd/balance/tally.go`: `rivalTally`, `tableTally`, `booksTally`, `crewTally`, `launderTally`, `lawTally`, `tradeTally`, `intelTally`), so a shadowed `:=` like #273's frozen/raids zero cannot compile; `TestEveryPolicyPlays` plays every registered policy five days and `TestPolicyListsMatchTheRegistry` holds this list and CLAUDE.md's count to the registry.
`-lielow N` overrides the lie-low threshold of the managed-style policies; `-corners N` caps how many corners `territory` and `war` work (`crewed` works as many as it can staff); `-force warn|push|hit` is how hard `war` sends the enforcers; `-rival P` forces the first faction's personality; `-factions N` (#43) puts exactly N factions in the run (1 is the duel the game was before the table, byte-for-byte: `harness.OneFaction`, `harness.Factions(cfg, n)`; 0, the default, is the file's 3..6 by seed), and every run with a table prints `factions: map[count: runs]; day 30 corners f1 3 f2 0 …; N pushes between factions (M corners changed hands), K absorbed, L leaders taken, P of your crew poached (Q offers), $H homage over D days (totals); dominant on N days`; the `diplomat`, `war`, `boss`, `saboteur` and `tipster` policies deal with `harness.Nearest(w)`, the faction alive at home that took a corner off them most recently, else the one holding the most there, else the rival at home; `-cash N` starts every run with that much dirty cash (`-policy hide -cash 5000000` is the rich player who sits on the pile); `-own a,b` starts every run owning those upgrades for free, prerequisites first (`harness.Own`).
`upgraded` is `crewed` plus the tree (#117: the cheapest node it can buy at 3x the price in hand, one a day, walking the branches in `harness.TreeOrder`: Security, Operations, Legal, Crew, then Laundering, which waits for a front the way an accountant does, since a bookkeeper with nothing to wash cost the crewed player $300k for nothing, Street and, once a route is on, Logistics, since #119's road nodes do nothing for a player who never runs the road and `crewed` plus the tree never buys them; `harness.BuyUpgrades`, which `distributor` and, at `BossMargin`, `boss` share).
`laundered` is `crewed` plus fronts: it buys the cheapest front it lacks when dirty cash is 3x the price, runs the launder dial at normal, drops to careful for 30 days after an audit and never buys a level (#192).
`distributor` turns the biggest route into home to normal on day one and keeps its target at `harness.DistributorDays` of home's demand for every product a lot undercuts home's street by `DistributorMargin` (the logistics sim buys the lots and sends them, #61); it is `laundered` until the wholesaler deals with it, then moves to Bayport for good, keeps runners on the corners at home and the rest on the corners there, restocks the corners there at retail, sells everything at home and there, and buys the upgrade tree as `upgraded` does.
`-cards decline|first` deals the dilemma cards and answers every one with its last or first choice; without it the harness plays with the deck in the box.
`-incidents on|off` (#44) deals the world's incident table or boxes it: on by default, so the tool plays the game as played and prints `incidents: N per run, M of 11 in the table seen: …` (and `-trace` a `day N incident …` line for each), off for a pinned number, since every harness test runs with it boxed (`harness.Run`, `RunFrom` and `RunWith` box it as they box the deck; `harness.Play(cfg, w, days, policy, Options{Cards, Incidents})` is the general form the tool and `TestEveryIncidentFires` use).
`diplomat` is `territory` that proposes a truce whenever the rival has taken a corner off it in the last `harness.DiplomatDays` and pays tribute once the truce has been refused twice; it stays at home, where the rival is (`distributor` leaves for the hub).
`delegated` is `distributor` that hires the first lieutenant looking for work (firing its least skilled runner to make room), gives them home, keeps `harness.HubCorners` runners on the hub's corners and leaves the rest idle for the lieutenant to post, fills the roster their people make room for, pays them off when their loyalty nears the flip line, and never posts or sells at home itself (the route feeds home either way); `-lt violent|greedy|careful|steady` forces the temper of the lieutenant it hires (by default they come as generated).
`funded` is `laundered` that gives the city it is in a quarter of its clean cash whenever the pressure there is over `harness.FundPressure` (60), up to what fills goodwill, and puts the same quarter behind the reform ticket in every city the day its campaign opens (#193, `harness.backReform`, up to what buys the whole swing; `boss` does both, paying every city); the `campaigns:` line prints campaigns backed, won and the clean cash behind them per run; `corrupt` (#42) is `distributor` that pays the chief `chief_price` whenever the heat where it stands or at home is over `harness.CorruptHeat` (50) and the chief is not bought (leaving one alone once observed zealous or once an envelope came back) and keeps the checkpoint or customs agent on its route bought, `CorruptMargin` (3x) the price in hand; `warlord` (#229) is `crewed` with the war order (`World.DeclareWar`) on the nearest faction alive (`Nearest`) whenever no war is on, the order as the scripted player plays it (`TestWarlordFightsMore` logs its corners won and its endings against `crewed`); `favoured` (#228) is `corrupt` that calls in the favour (`World.CallFavour`) on every morning the bought chief owes one and `heat.Sim.Due` says a sting, a raid or the task force comes tonight, the lever the favour is measured on (`TestAggressiveStillIndicted`); the `bribes:` line prints the envelopes and their cash per run, the backfires, the leads, the favours called in, and the checkpoints and their cash; `-chief corrupt|zealous|lazy` and `-da law_and_order|moderate|reform` force the law for the whole run (`harness.Appoint`: the actor is renamed and its clock stopped, so no election or term end replaces it), and the end medians print pressure and goodwill.
`boss` (#60) is `delegated` that plays the whole game: it works every corner in the hub, keeps `Territory`'s share of the roster as enforcers and sends them in at push only when the odds the picker shows clear `harness.BossOdds` (0.5; at push that is rare, and a hit at the same line wins corners and loses the run), otherwise talks the way `diplomat` does; sells and routes only the products worth their heat (`harness.BossWorth`: price per point of heat at least a fifth of the best on offer, so the street half of the ladder is dropped once designer is), keeps the route's target at the road's days of demand plus two, buys fronts and the tree at `harness.BossMargin` (1.5x the price in hand), pays generous, fires a lieutenant the morning their standing orders show the aggressive dial, and invests its clean cash in the fronts' levels (#192, `harness.InvestOver`: the cheapest next level of any front it owns, one a day, when the clean cash over a campaign's worth, `law.toml [campaign]` `Fill()`, is `BossMargin` times the price, after the town has been paid; `-margin N` is the levels' margin alone, `harness.BossAt`, the fronts and the nodes staying at `BossMargin` so the road is not starved; 1 invests everything, `TestInvestingEverything`), which is what moved its tier-4 row from $81M to $86M and its tier-3 row from $17.7M to $16.7M; `laundered` never invests, so tier 3 keeps its number; it reserves a lot a day offshore (#195, `harness.ReserveLot(ld, w, keep)`) over the same campaign's worth, and never retires; every run with a level bought prints a `fronts:` line, the levels owned, the clean invested and earned, the fronts shut for upkeep per run and the legit income a day at the end (means), and every run with a dollar offshore an `offshore:` line, the account and the fees per run, the lots over the line, how many retired and the median account at exit; the `endings:` line (#49) is how every run ended, `map[cause: runs]` with `still free` for a run going on at the horizon, then `N of M ended, scoring $X (median)`, the median `Stats.Score` (the offshore account over one plus the bodies, `docs/endings.md`) over the runs that ended.
`harness.NoEndings(cfg)` boxes the four detectors (`TestNoEndingIsTheOldRun`), and `BuyUpgrades` never buys the `identity` node: an exit is a decision a policy makes, and a test that wants it owns it.
`retiree` (#195, #49's policy, `harness.Retiree`) is `laundered` that reserves a lot a day keeping a day's upkeep in the pile and, once the account holds `retire_cash` after `harness.RetireAfter` (tier 3's checkpoint), recalls the crew and lies low until the days are quiet enough, then retires: 37 of 50 seeds by day 200, scoring the account (`TestRetireeRetires`).
`-undercut quiet|normal|aggressive` is the dial `pricewar` undercuts at (#68: `territory` that undercuts the biggest rival corner next door every day it can instead of striking; every run prints the undercuts, the units they moved, the corners the rival gave up and its muscle at the end); `leveraged` (#72) is `crewed` that takes whatever it restocks from the cheapest connect where it stands on their book first, up to what they will run it, and pays cash for the rest (the debt is collected on its day by the market sim), `-credit off` withdraws every connect's credit (`harness.NoCredit`; leveraged is then crewed exactly), and the output's `suppliers:` line prints the median relationship with the street connect at the end, the days in debt, the late payments, the freezes, the collections and the credit taken per run.
`stashed` (#73) is `laundered` that keeps its stock in stash houses: the cheapest house on offer where it stands once dirty cash is `harness.HouseMargin` (3x) the price and clean cash covers `RentCover` (30) days of the rent, the next (up to `-houses N`, default `harness.StashHouses`, 3; `-houses 1` keeps everything in one) once one there is over `HouseFull` (0.8) of its capacity, moving stock between them after the day's buying so none holds more than `Spread` (0.6) of the city's, and `-fronts off` makes it `crewed` with the houses, so nothing pays the rent; the output's `houses:` line prints the houses held and lost, the rent and the units lost out of them per run.
`-rival none` keeps the rival out of a run (`harness.NoRival`), `-heat off` switches heat off (`harness.NoHeat`: no source adds any and the ladder is out of reach), `-pace off` gives the rival its flat pre-#60 pace (`harness.FlatPace`), and every run prints the rival's median corners at `harness.PaceDays` (30, 60, 120) so the pace is a number in the output, and under it the rival's books at the same days (#139, `harness.RivalBooks`: `rival books: day 30 cash N income N/day wages N/day …`, medians of the chest, the take and the wage bill, so whether money can hurt it is a number too); the three boxes are how the ceilings a tier plays against are measured (`TestMoneyCeilings` logs them per row).
`saboteur` (#70) is `crewed` that works the rival's books instead of striking: it scouts whenever the snapshot is stale or unread, boosts the rival's biggest corner at the cheapest force whose odds clear `harness.SaboteurOdds` (0.5) while the war is under `SaboteurWar` (0.75) of the crackdown line (warn only under a deal), buys off a head at `SaboteurMargin` (5x) the price while the books as last read show a chest under `SaboteurThin` (3) days of its wage bill, and tips the police on that corner on the nights the rival holds more corners than it, no deal holds and the police are ready to act, keeping the tips up until the raid lands; `tipster` is `saboteur` that tips every night the rival holds a corner, the price of talking to police measured.
The output's `books:` line prints the scouts and the reads, the boosts landed and the cash they took, the tips and the raids they brought, the heads bought off, and the rival's muscle, its heat and your file at the end.
`cook` (#47) is `laundered` with a chemist: it hires the first one looking and cooks meth and designer toward the demand's share of the bag instead of buying them; `-cut R` wraps any policy in `harness.Cutter`, which returns `R / (1 + R)` of what the policy bought today to the connect and cuts the lot to put the same units back at nothing (the greed curve: `-policy managed -cut 0.5`); the output's `quality:` line prints the units cut in and their cost, the units cooked and theirs, the overdoses, the runs ending with a chemist, the mean quality sold and the median repeat business of the held corners at the end (`docs/quality.md`).
`cartel` (#48, `docs/assets.md`) is `boss` with the assets: it buys the cheapest open asset not owned first thing every morning its price is in clean cash (`harness.BuyAssets`), turns the plane and the tunnel on at the biggest open route's targets once they are open, hires a chemist, rents the houses at home and cooks what the file cooks in the lab once it stands (`labRoom`, `cookInLab`, the road's target for those coming down by what is cooking), and lies low the day a task force is announced; it is the money curve's tier-5 row, enforced at $100M–$1B since #205 ($167.1M on ten seeds at day 300 on c2d12af, $163.7M on the test's twenty, the boss $172.7M; the issue's $500M–$5B was three times what any policy reads, and #223 is the lever), and what is pinned beside it is the ordering, `TestCartelBeatsCorruptAndDistributor` ($181.3M against corrupt's $91.7M and distributor's $97.7M on ten seeds) and `TestCartelIsWithinFifteenPercentOfBoss` (-3.0% on the median, -8.8% to 0% a seed; a level pays 1% a day and a supply-side asset on a demand-bound operation does not, #205).
`reckless` is the cartel that never lies low and sells everything at the aggressive dial from the morning it owns its first asset (`harness.Reckless`): the one player who reaches the task force's line, and loses an asset for it on most seeds (`TestAggressiveCartelLosesItsAssets`).
The output's `assets:` line prints the assets bought and their cost, the upkeep, the seizures and the task forces per run, the tunnels found and what is owned at the end; `TestTaskForceNeverMeetsTierThree` and `TestNoAssetIsTheOldRun` pin that every other policy plays the ladder it always did.
`driven` (#46) is `distributor` with `harness.Drive`: the best driver looking for work is hired when the roster has room and put on the route with the most targets, so the same seeds read what the driver's cut is worth; `-life on|off` boxes `crew.toml [life]` (`harness.NoLife`: nobody ages, retires, is arrested, wounded or killed, no kin come looking; on by default in the tool and in every harness test, since the bands and the orderings are measured with it on), and every run with a body, an arrest, a wound, a retirement or a driven shipment prints `crew life: 2.3 bodies per run (1.4 yours), 3.3 arrests, 0.0 bails for $0, 3.8 wounded, 0.0 retired; 317 shipments driven, 11 of them seized (totals over N runs)` (`docs/crew-life.md`).
**The property** (#194, `docs/property.md`): `boss` buys the deed to the block under a corner of its own, cheapest first, one a day, at `BossMargin` over the campaign's worth it keeps, before the levels and never past the DA's line (`harness.BuyDeed(tr, w, margin, reserve, limit)`, `law.Sim.DeedLimit`); `harness.Landlord(cfg, policy)` wraps any policy with the deeds bought first at margin 1 with no reserve and no limit (the forfeiture's measure); `-deeds on|off` boxes `city.toml [deed]` (`harness.NoDeeds`: no block on sale, a run with it off is the run before the feature), and every run with a deed prints `property: N deeds held at the end per run (N bought, N seized by the DA), $N spent and $N paid back per run, $N/day rent at the end (means)`, `deeds N` on the trace line and a `day N deed …` / `day N forfeiture …` line each.
**The fronts' roles** (#344, `docs/laundering.md`): `harness.OneFront(cfg, lieLowAt, id)` is `laundered` buying the one kind `id` and no other (`washOnly`, which `washUpAt` is with no kind), the player `TestNoFrontDominates` plays once a kind; `harness.NoFrontRoles(cfg)` boxes `upgrades.toml [[front]]` (every front only washes) and `-roles on|off` is it on `cmd/balance`: a run with it off prints cb882df's output to the byte. `oldRunCase.file` plays a changed file against the box in place of the file as it is (the roles at the identity, `TestNoFrontEffectIsTheOldRun`).
**The characters** (#50, `docs/profile.md`): `-character C` starts every run as a character of `characters.toml` (`dealer | cook | bookkeeper | excop | dockhand`; `harness.Character(cfg, id)` is the `game.Start` handed to `sim.NewWorldWith`, `harness.RunAs(cfg, id, seed, days, policy)` plays `Run` as one, an unknown id is refused), `-hardda` seats a law-and-order DA and a zealous chief on day 0 without pinning them (`-chief` and `-da` pin), and the output's second line reads `character: cook (The Cook), hard DA false` whenever either is set; the harness measures a character with no profile at all, and `TestCharactersAreStartsNotCheats` runs the four difficulty tests as each character (the two orderings on the medians of ten seeds, at most one flip: a day-0 member takes a roster slot) and logs every median against the default's.

`informed` (#45, `harness.Informed`) is `diplomat` that pays a cop `cop_price` whenever the heat where it stands is over `InformedHeat` (50) and the file holds no cop's word on the police there that still speaks of a night to come (`InformedMargin`, 3× the price in hand), and lies low whatever the margin on a day the word says the police can move at the sting rung or above, so a sting that comes finds it not dealing and adds no page; it is the one policy that reads the file (`game.Known`), being the value of the file in numbers (`TestInformedOutlivesDiplomat`: twelve seeds lying low at `InformedTableHeat` 55, the diplomat indicted on day 83 and the informed player on day 134, $557k against $415k at day 200).
`harness.NoIntel` boxes the feed (no faction plants a lie); there is no flag, since the observation facts roll nothing and no policy reads them, and `TestNoIntelIsTheOldRun` plays the money curve's players on its twenty seeds on the file and under the box and finds the same runs (the old-run tests, below; until #276 it pinned t1–t4 to the dollar).
Every run that paid a cop, planted a spy or was fed a lie, and every `informed` run, prints `intel: 15.2 cops paid for $75833 per run, 0.0 spies planted (0 found, 0 of them shot; 0 reports), 0.0 lies fed (0 bit) per run; 4.4 facts held at the end (over 12 runs)`.

**The policies' building blocks (#275).**
The roster moves every crewed policy makes are one helper each in `internal/harness/hiring.go`: `bestCandidate(w, role)` (the most skilled of a role looking for work, the first on a tie; `staff`, the road's crew step, `Plant`, `Drive`, `hireChemist`), `fireWorstRunner(w)` (the least skilled runner, the first on a tie, to make room; `staff`, the delegated player's lieutenant, `hireChemist`), `fireSkimmer(w, tun)` (the first member under the skim line, one a day) and `guardScore(w)` (where an idle enforcer stands: a contested corner at 10 plus its demand, else its risk).
Their tie-breaks and iteration order are the copies' they replaced, since who is signed or fired is what the pinned numbers read.
`distributor`, `delegated` and `boss` are one `road` (`internal/harness/road.go`, `newRoad`: the route into home with the most room from the wholesale city, the hub, the sims, built once) whose steps they compose in one order: `dialOn`, `target` (days of home's demand, `DistributorDays` or the boss's pipeline; the boss only what is `worth` the heat), `arrive` (the laundered day while the wholesaler will not deal, else to the hub), `spendFair` or `spendBoss`, `crew` (a `roadCrew` saying whether it delegates, fires a violent lieutenant, how many guards and how many hub corners of its own), `restock` or `restockOnly`, `sell`, and for the boss `war` deferred after all of it.
It was one `distribute` with a `delegate` and a `fight` flag; the steps are the same code in the same order, and every trace and report hash is byte-for-byte the one before.
The sims a policy reads (`crew.New`, `laundering.New`) keep nothing between days, so each policy builds them once and passes them down: `staff(cfg, cs, w, city, corners)`, `Drive(cfg, cs, w)`, `washUp(ld, w)`, `washUpAt(ld, w, margin)`, `hireChemist(cfg, cs, w)`.

**Knife-edge tests.**
Some acceptance tests pin another issue's mechanism on a single seed and flip under any perturbation of the crew (a kin face is an extra candidate on 35% of hires, a wounded guard comes off a corner, a jailed runner sells nothing) while what they measure holds in aggregate.
Since #46 they run under `harness.NoLife` with a one-line comment each naming the seed and why, and life stays on everywhere else (the bands, the orderings, the digest, determinism and the save, every #46 test): `TestLieutenantSellsWhileYouAreAway`, `TestLieutenantsWantTwoCities` and `TestLieutenantKeepsTheCityStocked` (seed 2: the expansionist wipes the delegated player before it holds a second city; delegated gets a lieutenant on 8 of 10 seeds with life on against 9 of 10 without, its median 200-day net worth 56.5M against 50.8M), `TestTellIsAnswerable` (seed 4, day 42: the tell went unanswered with the outbidder's runner in a cell), `TestWarTakesGroundAndTheRivalTipsPolice` (ten push wars had exactly one crackdown on main and none with the wounded off the strikes), `TestLevelsDrawTheAuditors` (#192's greed pin: 36 audits against 34 and notoriety 997 against 996 with life on, the levels' signal under the crew's noise; the with-life figures are logged beside the pin so the next tuning pass can see whether `audit_level` needs to rise), `TestReputationCannotMaxAllThree` (its three hand-built enforcers die over 200 nightly hits) and `TestCookBeatsBuying` (seed 2 fires its chemists for loyalty and ends with none).
The rule stands: do not loosen these; box what they do not measure.
**The table (#43) is the same pattern**: with three to six factions on by default the seed-pinned mechanism tests of other issues run under `harness.OneFaction` too, a one-line comment each, while the bands, the orderings, determinism and the saves run on the table (and `TestUpgradedBeatsCrewed`, `TestManagedNormalBeatsQuiet`, `TestAlwaysQuietStaysFreeAndEarnsLess`, `TestCrewedBeatsManaged`, `TestLaunderedBeatsCrewed`, `TestDistributorBeatsLaundered`, `TestDealerBeatsCrewed` and `TestDiplomatHoldsMoreThanPassive` all hold on it; `table_share` and the shared push pace are what put upgraded back over crewed, from $752k against $900k).
Boxed for the table: `TestBooksInvariants` and `TestBooksAreDeterministicAndSave` (the saboteur reads the nearest faction's books, the test the rival at home's), `TestDefensiveNeverBetrays` (a chaotic second faction breaks its own truce), `TestSplitKeepsTheLine` (a split binds one faction, and another set up past the line), `TestLevelsDrawTheAuditors` and `TestLieutenantKeepsTheCityStocked` (seeds 5 and 6 with the boss never in the paper and no lieutenant hired), `TestDriverCutsSeizures`, `TestPricewarKeepsThePeace`, `TestPricewarIsAnswered` and `TestPricewarCostsMargin` (the price war's answers are one rival's temper), `TestReputationSourcesAndHeadlines` (an idle player's corner was pushed on by the second faction: fear 1.8), `TestRivalEconomyBinds`, `TestPricewarStarvesTheMuscle` and `TestTributeShareOfTheTake` (one rival's books, the middle cut 0.11x with its corners shared out), `TestTellIsAnswerable` (the outbidder answers the rival at home's tell and a second faction set up past it), `TestRelationshipPays` (seed 2), `TestLosingRunnersLosesCorners` (its operation never built), `TestNoUnlockIsTheOldRun` (main's digests), `TestStreetBranchSlowsTheRivalNeverStopsIt` and `TestLogisticsBranchMovesMoreForLess`, `TestSeedDigest` (the digest pins the sims on the duel's dice), and the survival loop of `TestIncidentsAreWeather` (seed 5's quiet trader, undercut by a second neighbour into a hotter product, is indicted on day 171 with the weather on; two seeds in forty, where main's own weather indicted one in forty; its bands stay on the table).
`TestRivalInvariants` runs on the table and holds #12's invariants per faction.

**The old-run tests (#276).**
A feature that a run never uses leaves the run byte-for-byte the run before it (CLAUDE.md, the day loop), and every feature since #192 has a `TestNo*IsTheOldRun` that says so.
Eleven of them are one helper, `assertOldRun(t, oldRunCase{...})` in `harness/oldrun_test.go`: every policy of the case plays seeds `1..seeds`, `days` deep, on the file and on `box(file)`, one parallel subtest a policy and a seed (`policy/seed1`), the world hashed each day with `digest` (`TestSeedDigest`'s walk), and the two lists must match to the last day, both runs ending the same day.
The case says only what differs: `never` (what no policy does, for the messages), `box` (`NoBondsman`, `NoDeeds`, `NoFavour`, `NoTax`, `NoWar`, `noLevels`, `noOffshore`, `NoEndings`, `NoIntel`, `NoLife`, `OneFaction`), `policies`, `seeds` and `days`, `forbid` (an event no run may emit, on either side), `scrub` (the fields the feature legitimately moves on its own, zeroed for the digest and put back: the favour's `Law.Favours` and the morning report, the account's `QuietDays`, the endings' `LegitDays`, logged while it counts), `after` (each run's world at the end), `until` (stop comparing the day the feature is on the ground in the file's run), `asRun` (play as `harness.Run` does, the deck and the weather boxed; unset, the raw file plays, the deck dealt and never answered and the weather on, as the eight hand-written copies did), and `every` (take the digest every Nth day and the last, the net worth alone between: the reflective walk is most of what a run costs, 20 s of 26 s CPU on twenty seeds daily).
The eight copies had drifted (one seed to three, 120 days or `Horizon`, subtests or not) and each keeps what it played: bondsman, deeds, levels and the account three seeds to day 120, the favour two to `Horizon`, the tax and the endings one to `Horizon`, the war one to day 120; unifying them on three seeds to `Horizon` doubled their CPU (33.6 s to 66.5 s).
`TestNoIntelIsTheOldRun`, `TestNoLifeIsTheOldRun` and `TestOneFactionIsTheOldRun` pinned the money curve's medians to the dollar (a copy of `TestMoneyCurve` every balance PR had to edit); since #276 they compare the box with the file, and `TestMoneyCurve` (the bands) and `TestSeedDigest` (the duel's sixty days, byte for byte) are the only exact pins: intel plays managed to the tier-1 checkpoint, crewed to day 120 and the boss to tier 4's on the twenty seeds, `every` 10, no cop, spy or bite; life plays managed (who hires nobody: life acts only through the crew) on the twenty seeds to `Horizon` with the hiring pool set aside (its faces carry the life stream's ages and kin), and the crewed (three seeds, 120 days) and boss (three, `Horizon`) players under the box carry none of #46's events, stats or fields; the table plays managed and crewed on the twenty seeds to the tier-2 checkpoint, the other seats set aside, until the second faction moves in or the table acts on you (`tableActed`: a push between factions, an absorption, a poach, trust spread, a homage offered; 9 to 67 days for crewed, 34 for managed), and every home policy's duel carries none of the table's state or events.

**Parallel tests (#212).**
The harness's tests run beside each other: `t.Parallel()` is the first line of every test, since each is a pure function of its own `content.MustLoad()`, its own `sim.NewWorld(cfg, seed)` and the seed's dice, and the three long tables are parallel subtests with the same seeds, days and assertions (`TestMoneyCeilings` one cell a row and box, sixteen; `TestCorruptChiefTable` its four chief × policy cells, the two comparisons read after the group joins; `TestEveryIncidentFires` eight chunks of twenty-five seeds sharing an `incidentTally` behind a mutex that only its `record` holds, released by a `defer`, so a `Fatal` in a chunk cannot leave it held).
A test may share nothing: no config another test could write (a test loads its own, or takes a copy from `OneFaction`, `NoLife`, `NoRival`, `NoHeat`, `Factions`, `Appoint`, which copy and never touch the original; `Play` copies once more before it boxes the deck and the table), no world, no seed, no package-level variable it writes and no environment.
The package-level state is read-only (`TierDays`, `RetireAfter`, `PaceDays`, `TreeOrder`, `moneyCurve`, `seedDigest`); `time.Now` is nowhere in `game`, `sim`, `content`, `harness` or `cmd/balance` (#50, `TestNoWallClockInTheSims`; the UI passes a date in); a config cache or a memo would race, and CI runs the harness without the race detector (#211), so `TestHarnessTestsShareNothing` is the guard: it type-checks the package and fails on any function that assigns to or takes the address of a package-level variable, on any package-level variable that holds a `content.Config` in any shape, and on any test that neither opens with `t.Parallel()` nor calls `t.Setenv`.
The twenty-three that call `t.Setenv("KINGPIN_HOME", t.TempDir())` stay serial (#50's `TestProfileNeverTouchesTheRun` among them, a full profile on disk being what it boxes), since `Setenv` panics under `Parallel`: the save round trips (`TestAssetsSaveAndReplay`, `TestBooksAreDeterministicAndSave`, `TestBribesSurviveSave`, `TestCampaignSurvivesSave`, `TestCardSurvivesSave`, `TestDeedsSurviveSaveAndDetermine`, `TestDiplomacyIsDeterministicAndSaves`, `TestDistributorIsDeterministicAndSaves`, `TestIncidentStreamIsTheSeeds`, `TestLawSurvivesSave`, `TestLevelsSurviveASave`, `TestLieutenantSurvivesSave`, `TestPricewarIsDeterministicAndSaves`, `TestQualityIsDeterministicAndSaves`, `TestRouteDaysTargetFollowsDemand`, `TestSaveKeepsContracts`, `TestSaveMigratesTheSuppliers`, `TestSixFactionsAreDeterministicAndSave`, `TestStandingIsDeterministicAndSaves`, `TestStashedIsDeterministicAndSaves`, `TestSupplyIsDeterministicAndSaves`, `TestTheAccountSurvivesASave`); the runner finishes them first (0.6 s together), so the wall is the parallel tail.
Nothing a test asserts moved: `TestSeedDigest`, every `TestNo*IsTheOldRun`, the bands and the orderings are byte-for-byte checks on their own world and dice, and parallelism cannot reach them.
Measured on an 18-core M5 Max at main 8b22ab1, `go test -count=1 ./internal/harness` wall, serial → parallel: at `GOMAXPROCS=4` (the CI runner's width) 30.0 s → 13.6 s plain and 271 s → 86 s under `-race`; at 18 procs 37.2 s → 7.1 s and 280 s → 41.5 s; `-race -count=2` reports nothing.
CI's 4-vCPU runner is about 2.1× slower a core (#209), so it pays the plain column (#211).

**The race detector (#211).**
On a pull request CI runs `internal/harness` and `internal/ui` without `-race` and every other package with it (the test step, in `.github/actions/go` since #276, two `go test` lines: the two plain, then `go list ./... | grep -v -e /internal/harness$ -e /internal/ui$` under `-race`, so `internal/ui/anim`, `theme` and `sparkline` keep the detector).
The detector reports a conflict between two goroutines and nothing else; the tree's own code has no `go` statement, no channel and no `sync` or `sync/atomic` use except the one mutex round `ui/anim/canvas.go`'s style cache, no test starts a goroutine, and Bubble Tea's runtime (`tea.Program`, started by `cmd/kingpin` and `cmd/anim`) is never started under `go test` (the UI tests call `Model.Update` and `View` directly; a `tea.Cmd` is returned, never run), so on today's tree the detector can report nothing anywhere while the harness paid 7.4× for it (537 s of a 555 s test step on #210's head, 92 s on the UI; #209's investigation).
The exemption is guarded: `TestNoGoroutineInTheTree` (`race_test.go`) parses every `.go` file under `internal/` and fails on a `go` statement, a channel type, send, receive or `select` in any file (test files included: a parallel test that starts a goroutine is what the detector would have caught) and on a `sync` or `sync/atomic` import in any non-test file, with `internal/ui/anim/canvas.go` the one file allowed the import (its package stays under `-race`) and a test file allowed `sync` for #212's tally behind a mutex (the chunks are the testing package's goroutines; `TestHarnessTestsShareNothing` guards what they share).
The detector still sweeps the whole tree: `.github/workflows/race.yml` runs `go test -race -count=1 -timeout 60m ./...` on `main` every Monday (`schedule`) and on demand (`workflow_dispatch`); it never gates a PR.
The ruling: the PR that first adds a goroutine to `internal/harness` or `internal/ui` (or a sim) names the file in the guard and puts `-race` back on that package in `.github/actions/go`'s test step in the same PR; until then a data race a PR introduces elsewhere is caught on the PR (its package runs under `-race`) and one in the two exempt packages cannot exist.
Measured on the PR's own CI run (34757774585) against #215's (34757109945, the harness in parallel but under `-race`): the Test job 2:13 from 6:56, the test step 106 s from 397 s, `internal/harness` 65.1 s from 384.3 s, `internal/ui` 27.3 s from 176.3 s, and the `-race` line over the other eighteen test packages 34 s with its compile (1.0–10.1 s a package); from #210's head, before #212, the job is 9:46 → 2:13 (4.4×).

**CI and the cache (#213).**
CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs on pull requests only, and GitHub scopes a cache saved during a PR's run to that PR alone, so with `actions/setup-go`'s own cache every PR built cold (`Cache is not found` on #204, #206, #208 and #210: `go build` 11 s, ~18 s to compile the harness test binary) and then saved two ~90 MB entries nobody read.
Since #213 a push to `main` runs `cache.yml`, which repeats `ci.yml`'s work, job for job and flag for flag (a `Lint cache` job, on Go `stable` until #276: `go vet`, staticcheck's install and run; a `Test cache` job on `go.mod`'s toolchain: `go build ./...` and the two test lines), and saves `~/.cache/go-build` (GOCACHE) and `~/go/pkg/mod` (GOMODCACHE; the lint entry adds `~/.cache/staticcheck`) with `actions/cache/save` under `go-test-<os>-<hashFiles(go.sum)>-<sha>` and `go-lint-…`; both workflows restore with `actions/cache/restore` on that key and the prefix without the commit as the `restore-keys`, so a PR (whose merge commit never matches exactly) takes the newest entry `main` saved for the same `go.sum`, and every push to `main` writes a fresh one instead of the once-per-`go.sum` entry setup-go keyed.
`setup-go` runs with `cache: false` in both.
`cache.yml` gates nothing and is not a required check: a red run there is a failure on `main` to look at; the entry is still saved on failure (`if: ${{ !cancelled() }}`; the build cache is good and Go never caches a failed test) and never on a cancel (`concurrency: cache-<ref>`, the newer push wins).
`ci.yml`'s test step dropped `-count=1` in the same PR so Go's test cache applies: Go keys a cached pass on the test binary's hash (so on every source, embedded TOML and dependency the package reaches), on the cacheable flags (`-timeout` among them, which is why `cache.yml` must run the identical lines: `go test -timeout 30m ./internal/harness ./internal/ui` and `go test -race -timeout 30m` over the rest, #211's split) and on every file and environment variable the test reads (`t.Setenv` and `t.TempDir` do not disturb it: the go command hashes its own environment and ignores files outside the module), so a PR that touches only docs or the UI reports the harness `(cached)` and one that reaches `internal/{game,sim,content,events,harness}` re-runs it in full.
That is sound here because the suite is deterministic by design (`TestDeterministicForSeed`, `TestSeedDigest`: a run is a function of its seed and its inputs, and those are the key), so a cached pass is the same evidence as a rerun; what it changes is that a flaky test is masked rather than fixed on a PR that does not reach it, which is what the weekly `race.yml` sweep and any `workflow_dispatch` run, both `-count=1`, are for.
Locally, `go test -timeout 30m ./...` twice reports every package `(cached)` the second time in 0.7 s.
A file a test opens *inside* the module is keyed on its mtime and size, not its content (`cmd/go/internal/test.hashOpen`), and a fresh checkout stamps every file with the checkout's time, so on the runner the five packages whose tests read the tree (`internal/harness`'s `TestHarnessTestsShareNothing`, `internal/ui`'s docs and key guards, `internal/sim`'s import guard, `internal/game`, `sim/rivals`) would miss on every run; both Test jobs therefore run a `content mtimes` step first that gives every tracked file an mtime that is a function of its blob (`git ls-files -s`: 28 bits of the sha above `@1000000000`, so 2001–2010 and never in the future, which Go refuses to cache) and every directory a fixed one, `.git` included (`TestStashHasNoWriters` walks the root, whose listing carries `.git`'s stat; the jobs run with `GOFLAGS=-buildvcs=false`, since the go command's VCS stamping runs `git status`, whose index rewrite moved `.git` again), which turns Go's mtime key into a content key: a same-size edit to `README.md` re-runs `internal/ui`, and a simulated fresh checkout leaves all twenty-two packages `(cached)`.
Measured on the hosted runner (4 vCPU): #215's Test job, the parallel harness under `-race` with a setup-go entry its own first run had saved, was 6:56 (build 3 s, test 6:37: harness 384 s, ui 176 s).
With #211's split and a cold cache (this PR's own run 34758908602, which restored nothing: `main` had no entry yet, and a `pull_request` run cannot read the head branch's entries, only the merge ref's and the base branch's) the Test job was 2:24 (build 12 s, test 1:59: harness 73 s, ui 30 s, then the `-race` line over the rest) and Lint 56 s (vet 17 s, staticcheck 25 s).
Warm, from `cache.yml`'s own runs on the branch, which are the same steps: with a merge that changed most packages (run 34758904474) the Test job was 2:09 with the save (build 4 s, test 1:44: harness 63 s, ui 27 s, the `-race` line 34 s) and Lint 40 s (vet 5 s, staticcheck 10 s); with nothing reaching a package (a push that touched only the workflows and docs, run 34759655277) the Test job was 0:24 with the save (build under a second, test 2 s: all nineteen packages `(cached)`) and Lint 25 s (vet 1 s, staticcheck 1 s).
The steady state for a PR, which restores and never saves, is therefore ~2:00 when it reaches a sim (the harness at ~65–75 s plain is the wall) and ~0:20 when it does not, the floor being checkout, `setup-go` (~9 s without its cache) and the restore (~3 s for a 78 MB test entry, 96 MB lint).
The first push to `main` after the merge writes the first `main` entries; until they exist a PR restores nothing and builds cold, as before.

**The pinned analysers and the one action (#276).** staticcheck and govulncheck are pinned in `go.mod`'s `tool` block (Go 1.24's `go get -tool`): `honnef.co/go/tools/cmd/staticcheck` and `golang.org/x/vuln/cmd/govulncheck` (the versions and the toolchain are in the next paragraph), run as `go tool staticcheck ./...` and `go tool govulncheck ./...` locally and in CI; the `go install …@latest` step is gone, so a lint run is the version `go.sum` names and a new staticcheck release can no longer turn a PR red on its own.
The tools' modules join `go.mod` as indirect requirements (`golang.org/x/tools` v0.1.12 → v0.30.0, `x/mod`, `x/sync`, `x/exp/typeparams`, `x/telemetry`, `x/vuln`, `honnef.co/go/tools`) and none of the game's own moves (`x/sys` v0.38.0 and `x/text` v0.3.8 stay; `go mod tidy` is stable over the result), so the build, every trace and every pinned number are the run before. govulncheck fails only on a vulnerability the code reaches (a symbol it calls); on Go 1.24.7 it reported none, and listed 7 in imported packages (all the standard library: `net/url`, `os`, `internal/syscall/unix`) and 39 in required modules (38 the standard library, one `golang.org/x/sys`) that nothing called, which the toolchain bump below clears.
The Lint job now runs on `go.mod`'s toolchain instead of `stable`: a pinned analyser is promised only the Go of its release, so the pin and the toolchain move together.
`.github/dependabot.yml` proposes weekly bumps for `gomod` (the tools included) and `github-actions` (the workflows under `/` and the composite action's directory, which Dependabot does not find on its own).
The steps `ci.yml` and `cache.yml` shared, and that had to stay byte for byte the same for a cached test pass to hit, live in one composite action, `.github/actions/go/action.yml`, with inputs `job` (`lint`: `setup-go` on `go.mod`, the `go-lint-…` restore, gofmt, `go mod tidy` drift, `go vet`, staticcheck, govulncheck; `test`: the `content mtimes` script, `setup-go`, the `go-test-…` restore, `go build ./...` and #211's two `go test` lines) and `save` (`"true"` in `cache.yml` only: the `actions/cache/save` step, `if: !cancelled()` as before); the keys, paths and flags are the ones above, unchanged.
`cache.yml`'s Lint cache therefore also runs gofmt, the tidy check and govulncheck (cheap, and gating nothing there).
What stays in the workflows: the Test jobs' `GOFLAGS=-buildvcs=false` (an action cannot set a job's environment, and `ci.yml`'s balance smoke runs after the action under it) and the balance smoke itself.
The action's `run` steps say `shell: bash`, which a composite action requires and which adds `-o pipefail` to a workflow's default `bash -e`; no line there has a pipe whose left side can fail on its own (the mtimes loop reads `git ls-files`; `go list | grep` is a command substitution in an argument list, whose status bash ignores).

**The toolchain and the module bump (#276).**
`go.mod` says `go 1.26.8` (no `toolchain` line: the `go` line is the toolchain `setup-go`'s `go-version-file` installs and the one a local `go` older than it downloads) since Go 1.24 left upstream support; Go 1.27.1 was out too, but 1.26 is still supported and the pinned tools' newest releases, staticcheck v0.8.1 (2026.2.1) and govulncheck v1.8.0, both declare `go 1.26.0`, so the Lint job's toolchain is the oldest their release promises.
The tools' own requirements moved with them (`golang.org/x/tools` v0.50.0, `x/mod` v0.41.0, `x/sync` v0.23.0, `x/sys` v0.38.0 → v0.48.0, `x/telemetry`), and `go get -u ./... && go mod tidy` took every module the game reaches to its newest: `charmbracelet/x/ansi` v0.11.6 → v0.11.8 (Dependabot's #287), `colorprofile` v0.4.3, `clipperhouse/displaywidth` v0.11.0 and `uax29/v2` v2.7.0 (`stringish` drops out), `go-colorful` v1.4.1, `go-isatty` v0.0.24, `go-runewidth` v0.0.30, `xo/terminfo` v1.2.0, `x/text` v0.42.0; bubbletea, bubbles, lipgloss, termenv and toml were already the newest of their major versions.
Nothing moved: the 24 balance traces and the hash of every daily report, journal and candidate pool are identical to `main`'s built on the same toolchain, and `main` built with 1.24.7 and with 1.26.8 gives the same traces and hash (amd64: Go fuses a multiply-add only on arm64, ppc64 and s390x, and the digest rounds to six decimals); `TestReadmeCaptures -update` and `cmd/keys -w` rewrite the README byte for byte, so the width libraries measure every glyph as before. staticcheck 2026.2 flags `parser.ParseDir` (deprecated since Go 1.25, SA1019), which `TestPanelsReadTheFile` now replaces with a glob and one `parser.ParseFile` a file. govulncheck v1.8.0 on the result reports no vulnerability at all, reached or not.

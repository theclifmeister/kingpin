# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## Workflow rules

Every change follows the same path. Do not skip steps.

1. **Start with an issue containing the plan.** Before writing code, open a GitHub issue (`gh issue create`) that states the goal, scope and acceptance criteria. Phase work references the design in issue #1 ("Part of #1"). If an issue already exists for the work, use it.
2. **One branch per issue, holding all of its changes.** Never commit to `main`. Branch from an up-to-date `main` and keep every change for that issue on that branch. `main` is protected: changes land only through a pull request with green CI.
3. **Link the PR to its issue.** The PR body must contain `Closes #N` for the issue it implements, so merging closes it. If a PR deliberately deviates from the issue's spec, say so in the PR.

CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs on pull requests only (never on `main`; it changes only through merged PRs) and checks gofmt, `go mod tidy` drift, `go vet`, staticcheck, `go build`, `go test -race`, and a short balance smoke run. Make the same checks pass locally before pushing.

## Commands

Go is installed via Homebrew and is not on the default shell PATH: prefix commands with `PATH=/opt/homebrew/bin:$HOME/go/bin:$PATH` or export it once.

```sh
go run ./cmd/kingpin                # play (needs a real terminal, >= 80x24)
go build ./... && go vet ./...
gofmt -l .                          # must print nothing
staticcheck ./...                   # go install honnef.co/go/tools/cmd/staticcheck@latest
go test -race ./...
go test ./internal/ui -run TestBuyThenSellFlow   # one test
go test ./internal/harness -run TestPriceInvariants -v

go run ./cmd/balance -policy managed -runs 50 -days 200   # headless balance numbers
go run ./cmd/balance -policy aggressive -seed 7 -trace    # per-day trace of one run
```

`cmd/balance` policies: `idle | hide | quiet | normal | aggressive | careful | managed | upgraded | crewed | territory | war | diplomat | laundered | funded | distributor | delegated | dealer | stocked | routine | boss`. An unknown policy silently falls back to `normal`. `-lielow N` overrides the lie-low threshold of the managed-style policies; `-corners N` caps how many corners `territory` and `war` work (`crewed` works as many as it can staff); `-force warn|push|hit` is how hard `war` sends the enforcers; `-rival P` forces the rival's personality; `-cash N` starts every run with that much dirty cash (`-policy hide -cash 5000000` is the rich player who sits on the pile); `-own a,b` starts every run owning those upgrades for free, prerequisites first (`harness.Own`). `upgraded` is `crewed` plus the tree (#117: the cheapest node it can buy at 3x the price in hand, one a day, walking the branches in `harness.TreeOrder`: Security, Operations, Legal, Crew, then Laundering, which waits for a front the way an accountant does, since a bookkeeper with nothing to wash cost the crewed player $300k for nothing, Street and, once a route is on, Logistics, since #119's road nodes do nothing for a player who never runs the road and `crewed` plus the tree never buys them; `harness.BuyUpgrades`, which `distributor` and, at `BossMargin`, `boss` share). `laundered` is `crewed` plus fronts: it buys the cheapest front it lacks when dirty cash is 3x the price, runs the launder dial at normal and drops to careful for 30 days after an audit. `distributor` turns the biggest route into home to normal on day one and keeps its target at `harness.DistributorDays` of home's demand for every product a lot undercuts home's street by `DistributorMargin` (the logistics sim buys the lots and sends them, #61); it is `laundered` until the wholesaler deals with it, then moves to Bayport for good, keeps runners on the corners at home and the rest on the corners there, restocks the corners there at retail, sells everything at home and there, and buys the upgrade tree as `upgraded` does. `-cards decline|first` deals the dilemma cards and answers every one with its last or first choice; without it the harness plays with the deck in the box. `diplomat` is `territory` that proposes a truce whenever the rival has taken a corner off it in the last `harness.DiplomatDays` and pays tribute once the truce has been refused twice; it stays at home, where the rival is (`distributor` leaves for the hub). `delegated` is `distributor` that hires the first lieutenant looking for work (firing its least skilled runner to make room), gives them home, keeps `harness.HubCorners` runners on the hub's corners and leaves the rest idle for the lieutenant to post, fills the roster their people make room for, pays them off when their loyalty nears the flip line, and never posts or sells at home itself (the route feeds home either way); `-lt violent|greedy|careful|steady` forces the temper of the lieutenant it hires (by default they come as generated). `funded` is `laundered` that gives the city it is in a quarter of its clean cash whenever the pressure there is over `harness.FundPressure` (60), up to what fills goodwill; `-chief corrupt|zealous|lazy` and `-da law_and_order|moderate|reform` force the law for the whole run (`harness.Appoint`: the actor is renamed and its clock stopped, so no election or term end replaces it), and the end medians print pressure and goodwill. `boss` (#60) is `delegated` that plays the whole game: it works every corner in the hub, keeps `Territory`'s share of the roster as enforcers and sends them in at push only when the odds the picker shows clear `harness.BossOdds` (0.5; at push that is rare, and a hit at the same line wins corners and loses the run), otherwise talks the way `diplomat` does; sells and routes only the products worth their heat (`harness.BossWorth`: price per point of heat at least a fifth of the best on offer, so the street half of the ladder is dropped once designer is), keeps the route's target at the road's days of demand plus two, buys fronts and the tree at `harness.BossMargin` (1.5x the price in hand), pays generous, and fires a lieutenant the morning their standing orders show the aggressive dial. `-rival none` keeps the rival out of a run (`harness.NoRival`), `-heat off` switches heat off (`harness.NoHeat`: no source adds any and the ladder is out of reach), `-pace off` gives the rival its flat pre-#60 pace (`harness.FlatPace`), and every run prints the rival's median corners at `harness.PaceDays` (30, 60, 120) so the pace is a number in the output; the three boxes are how the ceilings a tier plays against are measured (`TestMoneyCeilings` logs them per row).

Set `KINGPIN_HOME` to keep test saves out of your real config dir (tests do this with `t.TempDir()`). To drive the TUI headlessly, run it under `tmux` and use `send-keys` / `capture-pane`; `internal/ui/ui_test.go` has a `key()` helper for feeding `tea.KeyMsg`s to the model directly.

## Architecture

The game is a set of independent, deterministic simulations stepped once per in-game day over a shared `World`, with a Bubble Tea UI on top. The pieces are wired in `internal/ui/model.go` (`New`) and `internal/harness` (headless).

**Day loop.** `game.Clock.EndDay` (`internal/game/clock.go`) builds a `Tick` with a per-day RNG from `game.RNGFor(seed, day)`, steps every `Simulation` in the fixed order `market -> logistics -> territory -> rivals -> crew -> heat -> law -> laundering -> reputation -> news`, then clears the player's per-day scratch (orders, never the standing ones (#114), buys bar the morning's supply-contract receipts, which it keeps through the day for the cart (#113), lie-low, strike, hires, fires, upgrades bought, the proposal, accepted offers, abandoned corners, funding) and publishes the tick's events on the `events.Bus`. Because RNG is derived from `(seed, day)`, nothing about randomness is saved and a run is reproducible from its seed; `TestDeterministicForSeed`, `TestDistributorIsDeterministicAndSaves` and `TestSaveRoundTripIsDeterministic` enforce this. Sims must stay deterministic given `(World, Tick)`. `Tick.RNG` is the home city's stream; anything that happens away from home (another city's market and robberies, the road) rolls on a side stream, `Tick.Sub(name)`, derived from the seed, the day and the name, so a run that never leaves Eastside replays exactly as it did before the second city existed and every seed-pinned balance number survived #30 unchanged. Keep it that way: a sim that adds dice for the second city draws them from a `Sub`, and so does the law (`Tick.Sub("law")` for an election and a new chief), so the home stream survived #41 too.

**Events are the cross-sim channel.** Sims never call each other. A sim emits typed events (`internal/events/events.go`, each with a stable `Kind()`) via `Tick.Emit`; later sims in the same tick read `Tick.Events()` to react (territory, crew and heat consume the market's `PlayerSold`, the crew sim for a lieutenant's cut and the heat sim for their temper; crew applies the rival sim's `CornerStruck` toll to enforcers by nerve; heat adds the heat carried on `CornerStruck`, `RivalTippedPolice`, a crackdown `WarEscalated` and the logistics sim's `ShipmentSeized`; the law sim counts the violence, the hard product on `PlayerSold` and an `Enforcement` that shows the chief's hand; news consumes everything). After the tick, the bus delivers events to the UI (`Model.onEvent`) for the alerts and the journal. Adding an event kind means adding a headline template in `internal/content/headlines.toml` and listing it in `TestEveryEmittedEventHasTemplate`, unless it is report-only bookkeeping like `PriceMove`, `CrewPaid`, `RivalUndercut`, `WholesaleBought`, `SupplyBought`, `SupplyShort`, `StandingShort`, `ShipmentSent`, `ShipmentArrived`, `LieutenantActed` or `CityFunded`, or deliberately silent like `CrewTurnedInformant` and `LieutenantFlipped`.

**World is a plain struct** (`internal/game/world.go`) that sims mutate directly. Player actions (`Buy`, `PlaceSell`, `CancelSell`, `Travel`, `SetRoute`, `SetRouteTarget`, `SetLieLow`, `Hire`, `Fire`, `SetPay`, `Investigate`, `PayOff`, `BuyFront`, `SetLaunderDial`, `Assign`, `Unassign` in `actions.go`; `Fund` in `law.go`; `Post`, `Recall`, `Abandon`, `SendEnforcers`, `CallOff` in `territory.go`; `Propose`, `Withdraw`, `Accept`, `Decline` in `diplomacy.go`) only queue intent (or, for a buy and a trip, apply at once and are reported; the route dial and target are persistent settings like the pay dial); the market sim resolves sell orders during `EndDay`, the crew sim reports hires and fires, the territory sim reports claims, the rival sim resolves the strike and answers the proposal and seals the accepted offers, the laundering sim reports front purchases, the logistics sim runs the routes and moves the shipments, the law sim turns the day's funding into goodwill, and the morning `DayReport` is what the UI shows.

**Cities** (#30). `World.Cities` (keyed by id, `CityOrder` fixing the sequence, home first) holds what is per city: a `City` has its own `Market` (per-product `ProductMarket`, priced by `[city.market]` multipliers in `city.toml` on the ladder in `market.toml`, so coke is cheap in Bayport and weed is dear), its own `Corners`, its own `Heat` (0..100) and its static `HeatMul` and `Wholesale` flag. `World.Home()` is the first city (where a run starts and the only city the rival fights over: the rivals sim reads `w.Home().Corners`), `World.Here()` the one the player is in (`Player.Location`). Stock is a stash per city, `Player.Stash[city][product]` (`w.Stash(city)`, `w.Stock(city, id)`), and moves between cities only on the road, which the routes run; `World.Capacity(city)` is what the supplier there will sell into: the carry limit if the player is there, plus runners posted on corners there, plus idle runners wherever the player is, and `Free(city)` is what is left of it. `Buy` is from the supplier where the player stands, into the stash there; `Travel(city)` moves the player at once, recalls `You` from their corner (it drifts unless a runner takes it; the confirmation names it) and leaves the stock, and is for what only you can do there (stand on a corner, hire, ask around, sign a deal); `Post(corner, You)` is refused outside the player's city (`ErrElsewhere`), runners go anywhere. Sell orders are per city (`OrderKey(city, product)`, `w.Order`), resolved by the market against that city's worked corners, so a `PlayerSold` in a city the player is not in is the runners there (`TestSalesElsewhereAreRunnersOnly`). `Corner.City` says which city a corner is in; ids are unique across cities (content refuses otherwise) so `w.Corner(id)`, `w.PostOf(id)`, `Held()`, `Worked()` and `RivalHeld()` search every city, `WorkedIn`, `HeldShare` and `Demand` take a city, and `Borders` never crosses one. `PriceMove`, `PriceShock`, `PlayerSold`, `HeatChanged` and `Enforcement` carry the `City`. `SchemaVersion` is 9 (7 was the cities, 8 the rival's trust, 9 the chief and the DA): `Load` reads a pre-7 save's single-city fields (`Market`, `Territory.Corners`, `Player.Stock`, `Heat.Value`) off the stream a second time into an unexported `v6` record and `World.MigrateCities` wraps them into the home city (`logistics.Sim.Migrate` then lays out every other city in the config with every product the run has unlocked, and `territory.Sim.Migrate` runs again to seed corners for cities that have none), so an old save is a two-city world whose home city plays on identically (`TestSaveMigratesTheOneCity`, `TestOldSaveIsMigrated`).

**Corners are the demand.** Each city's `Corners` (seeded from `internal/content/city.toml`, one `[[city]]` per city with its `[[city.corner]]`s) each carry a size, per-product `Taste`, a heat multiplier and a robbery `Risk`. `ProductMarket.Demand` is per *standard corner* in that city; what the player can sell there is `World.Demand(city, product)` = that times `HeldShare(city, product)` (the summed shares of corners there that are `Worked()`: owned and with a runner, or `game.You`, posted). A held corner with nobody on it drifts back to `none` after `drift_days` (`territory.Sim.DriftDays(w)`: the file's plus the tree's `drift_days_bonus`, the corner boys; zero in the file is never and stays never; the map's `nobody, 3d left` reads it); an enforcer posted on a corner cuts its robbery chance (`territory.Sim.RobberyChance(w, c)`: `robbery_chance` times the corner's risk times the tree's `robbery_mul`, the watchmen and the dogs, and then the enforcer's cut, so the cut comes off the folded number); a robbery takes from the stash of the corner's city. The territory sim folds the tree once at the top of its step (`Sim.Effects`, #119) and reads those two names and no other. `Fire` and crew quits `Recall` the member so a corner never points at someone who is gone. The heat sim weights every unit sold by the corner it moves on (`CornerWeight`: corner heat, and `crew_heat` for a runner's corner) and by the city's `HeatMul`, so where you sell matters as much as how much. Dirty cash over `dirty_cash_threshold`, past what the fronts cover (`dirty_cash_cover` times what they cost: `heat.Sim.Cover`), is heat too, where the player is, but only dealing builds a case: a sting or raid that fires on a day the player attempted no sale in that city (no `PlayerSold` there with `Wanted > 0`; lying low resolves none) still costs stock and cash and cools heat but adds no evidence, so a cash pile draws stings without being a countdown (#27, `TestRichHiderIsNeverIndicted`). Heat is per city and the case is personal: every city's heat takes its own sales, decays on its own and holds the fear floor; the war's heat lands at home, an informant's, an audit's and the cash pile's where the player is; and the *hottest* city's police answer at the thresholds (one response a day, the cooldown ladder and `Evidence` global), taking stock from the stash there. `Heat.Peak` is the hottest any city has been.

**The law has actors** (#41, `internal/sim/law`, `World.Law`, `City.Pressure`, `City.Goodwill`, tuning in `law.toml`, names in `names.toml`). A **police chief** (`Chief{Name, Personality, Since, Observed}`, `corrupt | zealous | lazy`) and a **DA** (`DA{Name, Stance, ElectedDay}`, `law_and_order | moderate | reform`) are drawn from the day-0 RNG after the crew and the rival (`law.Sim.Seed`; schema 8 -> 9 seeds them on an old save from the current day, `law.Sim.Migrate`). They are tables the heat sim multiplies *its own* tuning by, read off `w.Law` through `content.LawConfig` and never the other way: the chief's `cooldown` scales `cooldown_days` for stings and raids (the patrol keeps its cadence under every chief: it fires as often as its cap lifts, and one that came back sooner would never lift, which is what stops managed play paying at all), `cap` what a patrol lets through and `decay` the daily decay (`heat.Sim.CooldownDays`, `PatrolCap`, `Decay`); the DA's `evidence_arrest` scales the pages an indictment needs (`heat.Sim.EvidenceArrest`, over the retainer's) and `sting` the sting line (`heat.Sim.Threshold`). #27 holds throughout: the law moves thresholds, cooldowns and decay and never adds a page (`TestQuietDayRuleHoldsUnderEveryLaw` runs the rich hider under every chief and DA). **Pressure** is per city, 0-100: the law sim, stepping after heat (so the police answer on *yesterday's* pressure, the way reputation reads yesterday's journal) and before laundering, adds `[pressure]` sources (`CornerStruck`, `RivalPushed`, a flip and a crackdown at home; hard product, `hard_products`, per `hard_units` sold in the city; yesterday's headlines about you, `headline_sources` as notoriety's, where you are), fades it toward `baseline` by `decay` of the gap, takes `goodwill_cut x Goodwill/100` off it, and reports a band crossed (`PressureShifted`, headlines `PressureShiftedUp/Down`). What pressure does, each sim reads off `w.Law`'s city through `[effects]` in its own config: the heat sim cuts the patrol, raid and arrest thresholds of the city whose police answer by `pressure_threshold_cut` at 100 and what a patrol there lets through by `pressure_cap_cut` (the sting line is the DA's alone: it is where a case starts), the rival sim raises its tip chance by `pressure_tip` (`rivals.Sim.TipPace`, the home city's). **Goodwill** is bought with `World.Fund(city, amount)`, clean cash only (`ErrNoCleanCash`, whatever the dirty pile), queued in `World.Funded` scratch and converted by the law sim at `goodwill_cash` a point (`CityFunded`, report-only), capped at 100 and fading by `goodwill_decay` a day; it is what 4.9's legitimate ending will read. **Terms**: the chief serves `chief_term` days and is replaced on schedule (`ChiefReplaced{Why: "term"}`), the report naming their personality only once `Observed` (`observe_days` in office, or any patrol, sting or raid); **elections** every `term_days`: the law-and-order share of the vote is `0.5 + (mean pressure - 50)/100 x election_swing` (`law.Sim.LawAndOrderShare`), a moderate takes `moderate` of every vote whatever the mood, one draw from `Tick.Sub("law")` decides it, a win on the sitting DA's ticket re-elects them by name (`DAElected{Incumbent}`, headline `DAReElected`), and a law-and-order DA elected with the mean over `replace_pressure` replaces the chief at once (`ChiefReplaced{Why: "da"}`, `ChiefReplacedDA`). A term of 0 stops the clock (`harness.Appoint` uses it to hold a forced chief or DA for a run). The dashboard's LAW panel (four lines: the chief and what they are like once observed with the days left, the DA and their ticket with the election countdown, the pressure bar where you are in heat's red with the goodwill, the other city's pressure; at 80 columns the days and the countdown go, `law-and-order` reads `law-order` and the last line is the rival's) sits beside RIVALS, the heat gauge's marks are `heat.Sim.ThresholdsIn(w, here)` so the ladder the player reads is the one the dice use, `f` on the ledger opens the fund dialog (`ui/law.go`, `modeFund`: left/right turn the city, an amount, blank fills goodwill to 100 from what is in hand), and the report has a LAW section (elections and new chiefs first). `harness/law_test.go` pins the acceptance: the aggressive trader is indicted sooner under a zealous chief than a lazy one and the hot crewed player sooner under a law-and-order DA than a reformer, pressure and goodwill stay in 0-100 under every policy, a hit war is louder than holding ground, the `funded` player ends quieter than `laundered` and gave only what its fronts washed, elections at pressure 80 return law-and-order more often than at 20 over 50 seeds and fire on schedule with a headline naming the winner, and the law survives a save. The harness policies that "pay attention" (`harness.TooHot`) lie low at the same fraction of the sting line as `heat.toml` prints it, and `RedMargin` lower once the file is within two pages of an indictment: the law moves the ladder the dashboard shows, and a scripted player calibrated to a fixed 55 would otherwise be stung on every seed that draws a zealous chief.

**Logistics is the road, and the road is a dial** (#30, #61, `internal/sim/logistics`, `World.Routes`, `World.Shipments`, `World.Logistics`, tuning in `routes.toml`). `[[route]]` edges join two cities with a `mode` (car, truck, boat), `days` in transit, `capacity` per shipment, `cost` per unit (dirty) and `risk` of interception per day in transit, and run from `from` to `to`: every route in the file runs from Bayport, the wholesale city, into Eastside. Shipping is not an action. Each route has a **setting** on the world, `World.Routes[id]` = `RouteSetting{Dial events.RouteDial, Target map[product]int, Days map[product]int}` (`w.Route(id)`; `HasTargets()`), set by `SetRoute(id, dial)`, `SetRouteTarget(id, product, units)` and `SetRouteDays(id, product, days)` in `actions.go` and persistent like the pay dial (a product keeps units or days of demand, never both: setting one clears the other, #115, and a map left empty is nil again, so a cleared route is the zero value and a pre-#115 save loads with `Days` nil, no schema bump): the dial is `off | slow | normal | fast` (`events.RouteDial`; off is the zero value, so a schema-9 save loads with every route off and needs no bump; `RouteDial.Ship()` is the `events.Ship` the shipment rides at, whose `[dial.*]` multiplies the days, never under one, and the risk on each, so `logistics.Sim.Days`, `DayRisk` and `Risk` are what the dice use and what the map shows; they take the world since #119, because the tree folds into them: `route_days_mul` on the days, still never under one, `route_risk_mul` on the risk of every day, `route_capacity_mul` on `Sim.Capacity`, `fare_mul` on `Sim.Fare`, a price per unit, so a dollar fare halves to fifty cents and a shipment's `Cost` is the fare over its units rounded up to the dollar, and `wholesale_mul` on `[wholesale] mul` in `Sim.Wholesale(w)`; `Sim.Effects(w)` is the fold, made once at the top of `Step` and again for every number the map, the pane and the target dialog show, so the odds the player reads are the ones the dice use), and the target is the stock the route keeps its destination at per product, as units, or as **days of demand** (#115): `logistics.Sim.Target(w, r, product)` is the units a target means this morning, the units as set or `days x World.Demand(to, product)` rounded up (`Sim.DaysTarget`, the same number the market screen shows as `demand/day`, which drifts with the street's price, so the units a days target means drift too; no dice), and `Shortfall` reads it, so holding more corners at the far end raises what the route sends with no change to the setting (`TestRouteDaysTargetFollowsDemand` in the harness, `TestDaysTargetIsDemand` in the sim). The target dialog (`ui/routes.go`, `modeTarget`) is three steps, product, then `units` / `days` on `←→` (the kind opens on what the product keeps), then the number (`numberField`, #112: `max` is the far end's `World.Capacity`, or for days the days of demand that holds), and on days says what they mean today (`Today  3d ≈ 180 units: ~60/day on your corners in Eastside`); the route's pane row, the target dialog's table and the ledger's LOGISTICS column read `target  3d (≈180) Weed · 400 Coke` (`targetLine`), and the LOGISTICS table drops `lost` and then `mode` (the pane carries both) where MAIN is too narrow for the targets. The harness's `distributor` still sets units by hand (`int(DistributorDays x demand)`, truncated, and a target of 0 for a product the lot does not undercut): a days target rounds up, so moving it would move every pinned distributor number. Every day, after the market has sold and before heat, the sim `move`s the road (rolls interception on every shipment in transit off the `logistics` side stream, lands what is due, `ShipmentArrived`, or loses it, `ShipmentSeized`, keeping `Logistics.Seizures` for the market and `Logistics.Lost[route]` for the ledger) and then `run`s the routes with no dice at all: for each route with its dial on, in file order, and each product in ladder order, the **shortfall** (`Sim.Shortfall`: target less the destination's stash less what is already `Bound` there) up to `capacity` goes today, out of the source stash first, then whole lots bought from the wholesaler at the source (`World.Restock`, the sim's, never the player's: `[wholesale]` sells `lot`-unit lots at `mul` of the street supplier price once peak cash reaches `unlock_cash`, into the stash regardless of its capacity, the odd lot's remainder waiting for tomorrow; `WholesaleBought`), then the fare; the road spends only what is over the **laundering float** (`Sim.Budget`, the same `float` the wash leaves in the till, passed to `logistics.New` and folded by the tree through `World.Float`, #118; the road never starves the street), lots before fares and never a lot it cannot then send. `World.Send` puts the shipment on the road (`Sent` the tick's day, `Arrives` the dial's days later, the fare off dirty cash, the units off the source stash, `Stats.Shipments`/`Shipped`), the sim emits `ShipmentSent` the same morning, and `Logistics.Days` keeps one `RouteDay` per route per day it spent anything (kept `record_days`, summed by `RouteSpend` for the ledger's week). A run with every route off draws nothing from the side stream and replays byte-for-byte as before the dial. Stock is conserved across a shipment and a seized one never arrives (`TestStockIsConservedAcrossShipments`); fast is seized more than slow (`TestFastIsSeizedMoreThanSlow`); the far city is kept at the target within `capacity` a day, nothing moves under the float and nothing with the dial off (`TestRouteDialKeepsTheTarget`, `TestRunKeepsTheTarget`); a seized shipment is a shortfall the route sends again tomorrow. A seizure is a market shock, not a bust (`TestSeizureIsShockNotEvidence`): the heat sim adds `seizure_heat` in both cities on the route and `seizure_evidence` only if the shipment was sent fast (#27: a case is built from what you did), and the market, which steps before logistics, reads the seizure off `Logistics.Seizures` the next morning and spikes the product in the city it was bound for by `shock_factor` for `shock_days` (`PriceShock.Seized`, headline `PriceShockSeized`); the report's SHIPMENTS block reads as a supply line (bought, left, landed, seized with the dial it was sent at, and each route's cost that day) and says, the first time, that a seizure is the cue to turn the dial down. `World.TotalStock()` counts the road, so wages never end a run while product is in transit; `NetWorth` values it at the destination's supplier price. Bayport's price multipliers and the lot are where the tier-4 margin comes from; `distributor` is the policy that runs the dial and `TestDistributorBeatsLaundered` pins that it pays. The Phase 4 hooks act on the route setting: corruption (#42) cuts a route's risk, the port and airstrip (#48) add capacity and routes, the driver (#46) is assigned to a route, an incident (#44) closes one.

**Fronts are the laundering.** `World.Fronts` (bought with dirty cash through `BuyFront`, priced by `laundering.Sim.Offers` from `internal/content/laundering.toml`, gated by `unlock_cash` like products) each wash `min(throughput x dial.mul, dirty cash over the float)` from dirty to clean every day, pay `upkeep` in clean cash (unpaid, the front shuts for `upkeep_freeze_days`) and roll an audit at `audit_risk x dial.risk`; an audit freezes the front for `audit_freeze_days`, seizes `audit_seize` of what it washed that day and emits `FrontAudited`. `World.Laundering.Dial` (`careful | normal | greedy`, `events.Launder`) is a persistent setting like the pay dial. The wash never takes dirty cash below `float`, so fronts can never starve the street of restock money (that is what "broke" means). **The laundering sim folds the tree** at the top of its step (#118, `laundering.New` takes it): `wash_mul` on every front's throughput, `audit_risk_mul` on `audit_risk x dial.risk`, `audit_seize_mul` on what an audit takes, `upkeep_mul` on a front's upkeep (`Sim.FrontUpkeep`, the ledger's), `audit_freeze_cut` off `audit_freeze_days` (`Sim.AuditFreezeDays`, never under a day) and `float_mul` on the float; the folded float is **one number on the world**, `World.Float(tree, base)` = `base x FloatMul` (the lowest owned wins), which the wash (`laundering.Sim.Float`, `Washable`) and the road (`logistics.Sim.Budget`) read, each passing its own copy of the tree and the file's float, so the two cannot disagree about where the street's restock money starts and a world owning no float node reads the file exactly (#113's supply contracts fold their own `[supply] float` through the same function: a contract is the street's restock, so the laundering float is what is left *for* it, not a line it keeps); the public reads (`Throughput`, `AuditRisk`, `Capacity`, `Upkeep`, `AnyAuditRisk`) fold for themselves so the ledger prints what the dice use. `TestLaunderingNodesPullTheirWay` (`sim/laundering`) probes every number the branch can move with each node owned alone and checks it moves the ones it names and no other; `TestLaunderingInvariantsUnderTheBranch` (harness) runs the laundered player with the whole branch and pins the pools, the folded capacity, the frozen fronts and that the wash and the road keep to the same halved float. Laundering steps *after* heat, so heat reads yesterday's audit off the front's `Audited`/`AuditDial` record the next morning: `audit_heat` in `heat.toml` every time, plus `audit_evidence` only if the front was being run greedy (#27: a case is built from what you do). Accountants (`[role.accountant]` in `crew.toml`, only in the hiring pool once a front is owned) add `accountant_throughput` to every front and cut audit risk by `accountant_risk_cut`, both scaled by skill; a skimming accountant takes from the previous day's wash, in clean cash (`CrewSkimmed.FromWash`). `NetWorth` counts clean cash, stock at supplier price and fronts at cost.

**Reputation is the player's face** (#14, `internal/sim/reputation`, `Player.Reputation`, tuning in `reputation.toml`): three axes 0-100, `Fear`, `Respect`, `Notoriety`, that the reputation sim drifts from the day's events and fades toward `baseline` by `decay` of the gap a day. The sim steps after laundering and before news, so it reads yesterday's journal and the band it crosses today (`ReputationShifted`, a multiple of `band`, up or down, six headline keys `Reputation<Axis>Up/Down`) is today's news. The street has only so much attention: past `total` between the three every axis shrinks in proportion, so you cannot max all three (`TestReputationCannotMaxAllThree` feeds every source for the horizon and the sum holds at 200). The effects live in `[effects]` as what the axis does at 100, scaling linearly (`content.Scale` / `content.Cut`), and each is read by the sim that owns the number, from its own copy of `content.ReputationFX` and `w.Player.Reputation`, never through another sim: the rival's push *chance* (`rivals.Sim.PushPace`, fear), its claim chance (`rivals.Sim.ClaimPace`, `rival_claim_cut`, fear) and what it makes of a proposal (`fear_deal`) and a kept deal-night (`respect_trust`), the crew's daily loyalty loss (`crew.Sim.LoyaltyLoss`, respect) and a candidate's signing fee (`crew.Sim.HireFee`, notoriety; fixed when the candidate is generated, so the pool catches up as it rotates), the supplier ratio (`market.Sim.SupplierRatio`, respect; the dashboard's supplier line reads it), and in the heat sim a floor heat decays toward and is never taken under (`heat.Sim.Floor`, fear) plus the weight of a unit *you* move on your own corner (`heat.Sim.PersonalHeat`, notoriety; a runner's units are theirs, so a notorious boss gets off the corner). The dashboard draws the three as one line of bars under the heat gauge (purple, blue, orange; the 80-column HEAT panel has no room for them) and the game-over summary reports them; `cmd/balance` prints the end medians. `reputation_test.go` in the harness pins the acceptance: every axis in 0-100 and the sum under `total` on every day of every policy, a feared player carries more heat than a respected one at the same volume, no policy ends with all three above 70, each effect pulls its way, and the save round-trips (`TestSaveKeepsReputation`). **The rival** (`internal/sim/rivals`, `World.Rival`, tuning in `rivals.toml`) is one faction per run with a leader, a personality (`expansionist / defensive / opportunist / chaotic`) and a starting trust in the player from the seed. It moves in on `arrive_day` on a corner away from you, earns off its corners, claims free ones by personality up to `max_share` of home's corners (`rivals.Sim.MaxCorners`: 0.6 of ten is six), pushes on the corners of yours it borders (`Corner.Borders`, `World.Contested`; odds from its muscle on the front line against `rivals.Sim.Guard`: an enforcer, you, a runner, plus the tree's `guard_bonus`, the front line, a body on every held corner, so the push odds the map shows fall on a bare corner and a guarded one alike and a defector's lead is no longer walked onto unopposed but pushes at those odds; and the push chance is `rivals.Sim.PushPace`, fear's cut times the tree's `rival_push_mul`, held ground; both fold through `rivals.Sim.Effects` where they are read, #119, and the strike odds and the claim pace read neither), undercuts you there (`Corner.Squeeze` cuts the corner's `Share`, plus a small street-price drag) and tips off the police while it holds a grudge. `SendEnforcers(corner, force)` is the player's answer, on the `events.Force` dial `warn / push / hit`: `rivals.Sim.Odds` is what the picker shows and what the dice use, heat scales with the corner, and every strike or push feeds `Rival.War`; past `crackdown_threshold` the police clear both sides' front line. A corner won by force is held with nobody on it, so post a runner before it drifts. `harness.Warlike` and `cmd/balance -policy war -force F -rival P` measure it; `rivals_test.go` in the harness pins the acceptance criteria (a passive player loses corners to an expansionist within 60 days, a hit war reaches the patrol line faster than aggressive selling, ownership / cash / muscle / squeeze invariants). The expansionist is the hard draw: it keeps pushing past its cap at half pace, so a player who never guards or fights loses ground for as long as they let it. **Its pace is data** (#60, `rivals.toml [pace]`, folded in from #69, which keeps the tell): its claim chance is scaled by the player's share of home's corners between `claim_scale_min` (0.25, nothing held) and `claim_scale_max` (1.5, all of it; `rivals.Sim.ClaimScale`), cut by fear (`reputation.toml rival_claim_cut`, `rivals.Sim.ClaimPace`, read the way `PushPace` reads `fear_push_cut`), and after any claim it waits `claim_cooldown` (12) days before it claims again unless the player's enforcers have been in within those days (`Rival.LastStruck`; `rivals.Sim.Rested`), its arrival not counting as a claim; for `arrive_grace` (5) days after it arrives it never sets up on a corner the player has ever held (`Corner.Yours`, stamped by the territory sim). The cooldown roll is still made, so the seed's dice do not move, and while it waits it pushes at `push_past_cap` as it does at its cap, because a slower spread must not be a longer fight at full pace (with pushes unpaced the expansionist took eight of the delegated player's corners on seed 2 where the flat pace took four). Against an expansionist a player holding half the city sees a median of 3 rival corners at day 30 (6 before) and 9 at day 120 (9 before); `cmd/balance` prints the medians and `-pace off` is the flat pace. **The claim is telegraphed** (#69, the tell): the day the claim roll succeeds the sim picks the corner (`pickFree`, as before) and *eyes* it (`Rival.Eyeing`, `Rival.EyeingDay`, `RivalEyeing{Corner}`, headline and report line, the reason named only once `Observed`), stamping `LastClaim` that day so the cooldown runs from the choice and the cadence #60 set is unchanged, and on the next step (`rivals.Sim.resolveEyeing`) sets up on it, `claim_cost` spent, unless the corner is held by then (a `Post` of you, a runner or an enforcer, the crew step's delegation counting since it runs between; an enforcer alone holds it, and the rival never walks onto held ground without a push), in which case the claim fails, nothing is spent, `Grudge` rises by `outbid_grudge` and `RivalOutbid{Corner}` is emitted, or dropped silently where it can no longer act (a split sealed overnight covers it, its share reached, routed). No new dice: the roll is the one made before, resolved a day later, so a pinned seed that reads a single day can shift (the claim lands a day later). The map marks the eyed corner `?` in `theme.RivalText` with `theirs tomorrow` in its cell, the inspector reads `they set up here tomorrow` with `c post a runner to keep them off`, the dashboard's RIVALS line (LAW's last line at 80 columns) and the rivals screen's leader line carry `eyeing Riverside` (`eyeingWord`; the tell outranks the temper, the count and the name where the line is short), and fast-forward stops on `RivalEyeing`, the one thing it exists to give the player a day for. `harness.Outbidder` / `harness.Outbid` is the scripted player that posts a runner on the eyed corner the morning of the tell; `TestTellIsAnswerable` pins that every claim then fails on every seed with the rival's cash unspent and the grudge risen, and `sim/rivals` pins the tell, the outbid's exact cash and the stale tells.

**Diplomacy is the table** (#32, `rivals.toml [diplomacy]` and `[deal.*]`, `game/diplomacy.go`, `sim/rivals/diplomacy.go`, `ui/diplomacy.go`): deals are state on the rival (`Rival.Trust` 0-100, `Rival.Deals`, `Rival.Betrayed`), offers are on the world (`World.Offers`), and everything is proposed, answered, kept and broken inside the rival's step. Three kinds ship: a **truce** (`Terms.Days`; no pushes, no undercutting, no tips, and it ends with `DealEnded` at the close of its last night), **tribute** (`Terms.PerDay`, dirty cash the rival step moves each night, `TributePaid`; it leaves every corner of yours alone until you stop paying) and a **split** (`Terms.Corners`, the ids on the player's side of the line: the rival neither claims nor pushes on them, `Post` refuses a free corner past the line, and `World.SplitLines` are the three lines the UI offers). A deal holds on ticks before `Until` (`Deal.Live`), `World.Deal(kind)` asks about the coming tick, and `World.AtPeace` is a truce or a tribute. Player actions queue intent: `Propose(kind, terms)` (one a day, checked for shape; `Withdraw`), `Accept(id)` (moves the offer to `World.Accepted` scratch, sealed the next morning on exactly the offered terms) and `Decline(id)`; `Abandon` records the corner in `World.Abandoned` scratch so the step can notice a split corner walked off. The rival's answer is `rivals.Sim.Chance`: the kind's `base`, the terms' `Favour` (-1 the hardest ask to 1 the easiest: a short truce, a fat tribute, a modest line; a split past `split_fair + split_trust x trust` or over a rival corner is refused outright) times `terms`, `accept_trust` at full trust, the personality's `deal_bias`, `accept_war` at a full war, and `fear_deal` from `reputation.toml [effects]`; zero for `distrust_days` after a betrayal (`Sim.Distrusted`) or on a night the enforcers go in. The dialog shows that number and the dice use it. The rival offers too (`offer`, one on the table at a time, `offer_days` to answer, `personality.offer_chance` per day its situation calls for one): an expansionist that cannot pay its muscle `low_cash_days` asks for a truce, an opportunist with `upper_hand` times the guard on the front line demands tribute at the middle cut, a defensive one asks for the long truce once the war is loud, a chaotic one with a front line asks for the short one. Every night a deal holds earns `trust_kept` (times `Scale(respect, respect_trust)`) and, for a truce or a split, `respect.deal_kept` in the reputation sim, which reads `w.Rival.Deals` after the rival step has ended what ran out; a strike costs `force.X.trust`. **Betrayal** is a push or a hit under any deal (every deal breaks), a missed tribute or a split corner abandoned: trust to `betrayal_floor`, `Rival.Betrayed` stamped, one `RivalTippedPolice` that night whatever else happens, `DealBroken{By: "you"}`; a chaotic rival breaks deals at `personality.betrayal` per deal-night (`DealBroken{By: "rival"}`, its trust untouched), the others never. **Snitching** (#13, `crew.toml [informant]`, `heat.toml informant_*`) is the crew sim and the heat sim talking through the world. A member under both the loyalty and the nerve line rolls daily to turn (`CrewMember.Informant`, the hidden flag; the roster never shows it, `Stats.Informants` counts them); the crew sim emits `CrewTurnedInformant`, which has no headline on purpose and which the heat sim consumes only to start its clock (`Heat.LeakDay`). From then on, every `informant_days`, the heat sim adds `informant_evidence` pages to `Heat.Evidence` and resets `Heat.EvidenceDay` whatever was sold and whatever the lawyer's `evidence_cut` says (a witness is not a bust), so an informant is the one exception to #27's rule and the reason the retainer's decay is worth having; it adds `informant_heat` that is deliberately left out of the `HeatChanged` reasons. The tells are therefore in the report: a "the DA's file on you grows" line under a heat delta with no sting or raid, and a delta the dial does not add up to. `Heat.Leaks` counts them, resets to zero once nobody on the payroll is talking, and at two the dashboard and crew screen hint "Somebody is talking." A raid while one is on the payroll takes the whole stash (`Enforcement.Stash`). The player's answers: `World.Investigate(cost)` queues a night's questions (`World.Investigation`, per-day scratch, paid up front from dirty then clean cash) that the crew sim resolves with `crew.Sim.InvestigateOdds` (base, plus the best enforcer's skill, plus `investigate_learn` per empty night in `Crew.Investigated`; the UI shows the same number), naming an informant into `Crew.Exposed` (the roster marks them SNITCH) or costing everyone `investigate_loyalty`; `World.PayOff(id, cost, loyalty)` buys loyalty at once, not silence; and `Fire`, which is the only thing that stops an informant, and which the crew do not hold against you when it was one (`CrewFired.Informant`). At the quit threshold a member walks as before unless the rival holds ground, in which case they defect (`CrewDefected`, `Rival.Leads`, `Stats.Defections`): the rival sim, next step, adds them to its muscle and walks onto the corner they ran if nobody stands there (`CornerTaken.Handed`), or pushes at the usual odds if somebody does; a corner in another city is no lead at all (the rival lives at home: the crew sim hands over none and the rival sim refuses one, `TestDefectionElsewhereHandsNoCorner`). `harness.Plant` puts an informant on the payroll from day 0, `harness.Vigilant` is the crewed player who investigates once the tell has shown twice and fires whoever is named, and `snitch_test.go` pins the acceptance: the always-quiet player with a plant is indicted within `harness.SnitchDays`, the vigilant one survives, a skill-90 enforcer names the informant within three nights on every seed and a skill-10 one takes materially longer, a defection is a rival corner gain the next day. An audit (#29) is the other way in: the laundering sim, stepping after heat, turns the least loyal accountant under the same loyalty line with no dice (`laundering.Sim.flip`, `TestAuditFlipsTheAccountant`), and because heat never sees that tick's event it stamps `LeakDay` every day nobody is talking, so the clock runs from the audit either way. None of the new world fields needed a schema bump: their zero values are the pre-#13 state.
**Lieutenants run a city for you** (#31, `crew.toml [lieutenant]`, `[lieutenant.personality.*]` and `[role.lieutenant]`; `sim/crew/lieutenant.go`; `ui/lieutenant.go`). A lieutenant is a crew member (`CrewMember.Personality`, fixed at generation from `violent / greedy / careful / steady` and hidden until `Observed`; `City`, `Assigned`) and everything they do is a policy over the actions the player has, run inside the crew step for each city that has one (`World.Assign(id, city)` / `Unassign`, one per city; `CrewState.Lieutenant(city)`): nothing new is simulated, only who decides. Candidates are lieutenants with `chance` only once the player holds corners in two cities (`crew.LieutenantsWanted`; the roll is only made then, so a one-city run draws the pool it always did), and an *assigned* lieutenant adds `[role.lieutenant].crew` slots to the roster (`crew.Sim.MaxCrew(w)`; `Hire` takes the cap from the caller), which is how a six-strong crew staffs a second city. Every night, after the roster has settled: the cut (`[role.lieutenant].cut` of their city's takings, `Stats.Cuts`) and a greedy one's `skim` on top, at any loyalty, come off dirty cash first (the skim is folded into the day's `CrewSkimmed`); under `flip` loyalty they turn informant with no dice (`LieutenantFlipped`, hidden like `CrewTurnedInformant`: the heat sim starts its clock on it and, while a lieutenant is talking, files `[lieutenant].evidence` pages a leak instead of `informant_evidence`); at `quit_threshold` one running a city **walks with it** (`crew.Sim.walk`: every corner held there bar the one you stand on goes to the rival if it holds ground in that city, else to the street, the stash there is emptied, `LieutenantWalked`, `Stats.Walked`); then delegation (`crew.Sim.delegate`, no dice): the crew come off a corner robbed twice since it was claimed (`Corner.Robbed`, counted by the territory sim and forgotten `drift_days` after the corner goes back to the street), idle runners go on the best corners there (a held one nobody works, else the biggest free one; `Post` refuses a split corner, so the next is tried, and a claim's `Since` is set to the tick so the territory sim reports it in the morning), idle enforcers guard the worked ones if the temper's `guard` says so, whatever is held with no runner is abandoned (never the player's own corner or one a live split covers), and every product in the stash gets a **standing order** at the temper's `dial` (`World.Delegated`, keyed like `Orders`, refreshed nightly and dropped on `Unassign`, `Fire` or a walk; `World.DelegatedOrder`; `World.StandingOrder` is the player's own standing order first, #114, then the lieutenant's). The market resolves the lieutenant's order only where the player placed no order of their own and no standing one (`PlayerSold.Standing` and `Delegated`), and every `PlayerSold` in a run city carries `Lieutenant` / `LieutenantName`, which the crew sim uses for the cut and the heat sim for the temper's `heat` multiplier (`heat.Sim.LieutenantHeat`, inside `SaleHeat` so the dial preview is honest). Lying low is everyone's day off. `LieutenantActed` is report-only bookkeeping for what they did and what it cost; the report names the temper once they have run a city for `reveal_days` (`Observed`). The zero values are the pre-#31 state, so no schema bump. Acceptance in `harness/lieutenant_test.go`: the delegated player never places an order at home once a lieutenant has it and home sells on standing orders (unassigned nightly, it sells nothing); on one seed and policy a violent lieutenant's city is hotter at day 60 than a careful one's, a greedy one keeps less of the same takings as a steady one and a careful one takes less; a lieutenant at loyalty 0 walks the next night with every corner unheld and the stash gone; the player's order for a product wins the day; a flip is dice-free and files thicker pages; lieutenants only come looking with two cities held; assignment, temper and standing orders survive a save and the run replays; and `delegated` at `steady` is within 20% of `distributor` at the horizon (`harness.Delegate` puts one on the payroll for a test). Balance: the lieutenant adds corners, not a heat budget or the rival's forbearance, so the tier-4 row is the boss's, not `delegated`'s (see `moneyCurve`); a violent one in a delegated run can raid the operation into an arrest, which is the temper working as designed under a policy too blunt to fire them (the boss reads the aggressive dial off their standing orders and fires them that morning).

**All tuning is data.** `internal/content/*.toml` is embedded and decoded into `content.Config`; sims take their config struct at construction (`sim.Default`). Balance changes should be TOML edits, verified with `cmd/balance` and the harness tests in `internal/harness/balance_test.go`, and `crew_test.go`, which pin the difficulty curve (always-aggressive gets indicted fast, always-quiet survives and earns less, managed beats quiet, crewed beats managed, upgraded beats crewed) and the crew invariants (loyalty monotone in pay, no skim above the threshold). `TestMoneyCurve` pins the economy's scale (issue #24): one row per progression tier giving the band the best policy's median net worth must land in at that tier's checkpoint (`harness.TierDays`): tier 1 `managed` ($85k at day 30, band $50k-$200k), tier 2 `crewed` ($580k at day 70, $500k-$2M), tier 3 `boss` ($18M at day 120, $5M-$20M) and tier 4 `boss` ($63M at day 200, $50M-$200M), all enforced since #60; the tier-5 row waits for #48. `TestMoneyCeilings` logs, per row, what the policy makes with the rival kept out, with heat off, and with both (`harness.NoRival`, `harness.NoHeat`), so a missed band names its wall: on `main` before #60 the tier-3 and tier-4 rows (`laundered` $1.0M, `delegated` $3.3M) could not reach their bands even with both boxed ($3.9M and $8.3M), which is what said the street itself was tier-1 sized. What #60 changed, and why (the tuning rules of thumb, continued): (1) **the ladder is the tiers**: heroin and meth are what tier 1 and 2 sell (heroin 14 a corner a day at heat 1.8, meth 3 at 2.2; per unit the ladder gets hotter, per dollar cooler, which is what climbing it is for), and designer is the port's product, forty a standard corner at heat 2.5, unlocked at the wholesaler's line and sold by Bayport's supplier only (`city.toml [city.market] designer = { no_supply = true }` at home, `ProductMarket.NoSupply`, `Buy` refuses with `ErrNotSupplied`, the market sim restamps the flag from the config every day so an old save follows the file), so it reaches home by the road and never through a tier-2 crew's supplier. A cash gate cannot do that job: `crewed` never spends, so its peak cash crosses $500k on day 66 and $700k on day 90, while the boss, which buys fronts, the tree and lots, crosses $700k on day 134; any gate that spares the tier-2 crew until day 120 shuts the tier-3 player out until day 130, and the $500k gate the boss clears by day 75 gave `crewed` designer from day 66 and an indictment by day 114. (2) **Heat scales with the operation, never with the lone trader**: the two tier-priced Security nodes `ghosts` ($250k) and `cutouts` ($1.5M) each multiply `crew_heat` by 0.4 (`crew_heat_mul`, a product; `heat.Sim.CrewHeat`), so a runner's unit at tier 4 draws 3% of one you move and the always-aggressive trader, who has no runners, is indicted exactly as fast with the whole branch (`TestSecurityBranchSoftensAggressive` is the guard: a `sale_heat_mul` or `street_units` node strong enough for tier 4 would take that trader under the sting line). (3) **Fronts cover the pile**: the wash ladder tops out near $400k a day and a tier-4 operation makes more, so `dirty_cash_cover` (10x what the fronts cost, on top of `dirty_cash_threshold`) is the pile the police read nothing into; a rich hider with no fronts is covered for nothing and #27 holds (`TestRichHiderIsNeverIndicted`). Without it the boss sat on $17M dirty at day 200 and lay low every other day for it. (4) A lieutenant brings eight people, not four (`[role.lieutenant] crew`), so a boss can work the hub's six corners itself and leave home fully staffed. (5) The rival's pace (below). Tier 1 and 2 moved +4% and -12%; every difficulty and acceptance test held, and the two fixtures that moved were `runCity` (every rung unlocked from day 0, so the temper that skims does not unlock a product a day later) and `TestReputationCannotMaxAllThree` ($1M in the bag instead of $10M: the bigger pile plus an aggressive dump of the new ladder was an arrest on day 3). `TestIdleMarketStaysNearEquilibrium` allows 25% because supply shocks (a slump cuts demand, not price) hold an idle market's mean about a fifth over base in every city. `laundering_test.go` pins the wash invariants (pools never negative, a day's wash never over capacity, frozen fronts wash nothing, greedy washes more and is audited more than careful, an audit is evidence only when greedy, laundered beats crewed). Day counts in the harness (`harness.Horizon`, `TierDays`, `-days`) are where the tooling *looks*, never a run length: the game has no day cap, a run ends only through an ending, and the player sets the pace. Do not add mechanics that end a run merely for playing on (#27 removed the one that did). `territory_test.go` pins the corner invariants: served demand is exactly the worked corners' share, losing every runner loses every corner within `drift_days`, and no corner means nothing sells. Products above the starting three are gated by `unlock_cash` in `market.toml`: the market sim lists a product the first day after peak cash reaches it, in every city, and a city's `no_supply` says its supplier does not sell it there (designer, the port's product). Sale heat is per unit moved (`sale_heat` per `street_units` heat-weighted units), so heat scales with volume, not with how many products exist.

**Dilemma cards** (#15, `internal/content/dilemmas.toml`, `game/dilemmas.go`, `sim/news/dilemmas.go`, `ui/card.go`) interrupt the day with a choice. The news sim, last in the order, deals at most one a day from the day's RNG: none for `min_gap` days after the last, then a chance rising each day to certainty at `max_gap` (one roll a day whatever happens, so the stream is fixed for the seed), then a weighted pick over the eligible cards with ones already drawn thinned by `1/(1+drawn)` and `once` cards retired. `[card.trigger]` fields (`content.CardTrigger`) must all hold, and the ones that name somebody fill the slots (`news.Slots`: the least loyal member under `loyalty_below`, the most loyal over `loyalty_above` or of `role`, the biggest worked corner for `corners`, the pair for `contested`, the first front) deterministically, no dice; `news.Eligible` is that check and `TestTriggersHold` tables it. The card is rendered when drawn (`game.Card`, text, choices and effects included) into `World.Dilemmas.Pending`, so a save on a card restores it without the content and `World.Choose(i)` resolves it: effects apply at once through `applyEffect`, the one function that knows every key in `game.EffectKeys` (a key it does not know is an error; `news.New` refuses a deck with one, so a typo fails at start-up), the outcome goes in the journal with source `dilemma`, and `Dilemmas.Answered` (per-day scratch) carries the optional follow-up headline for the news sim to print the next morning. Cash keys clamp at zero, heat/loyalty/war at 0..100; `*_amount` keys are multiples of the card's `Amount` (`amount_share` of dirty cash, at least `amount`, rounded to two figures) so a card can name a sum and then take it; `fear`, `respect`, `notoriety` move `Player.Reputation` at once, clamped to 0..100, and the reputation sim applies its sum cap that evening as it does to every source. The UI shows the card in `modeCard` before the report (`showCard`): `1-3`/`enter` decide, the outcome shows, a second `enter` opens the report, and `enter` never ends the day; `continueRun` reopens a pending card. The deck's convention is that the last choice is what doing nothing gets, and that is `harness.Decline`; `harness.Run`/`RunFrom` play with the deck boxed (the invariants they pin belong to the other sims), `RunWith(..., Chooser)` deals it and answers before the policy acts. `harness/dilemmas_test.go` pins determinism (same seed, same choices, same cards on the same days), a save on a card replaying identically, and every card in the deck being dealt and rendering without a hole; `news/dilemmas_test.go` pins the deck's shape, the pacing and the unknown-key refusal. The zero value of `DilemmaState` is the pre-#15 state, so no schema bump.

**Buyers are the market screen's reason to exist** (#71, `internal/content/buyers.toml`, `content.BuyersConfig`, `game/buyers.go`, `sim/market/buyers.go`, `ui/buyers.go`). A buyer is a named person in a city who wants product off-corner on a deadline at a premium; the deck is `[[buyer]]` entries with a `city` (or `any`), a `product` (or `any`, narrowed by `min_price` to a tier), bands for `size` (in days of the demand your corners in that city serve for the product, never under one standard corner's, so a buyer scales with the operation and a cornerless player still gets an order), `days` and `premium`, a `penalty` (respect) and `penalty_cash` (of the undelivered units' street value), a `heat` multiplier per unit, `weight`, `once`, `unlock_cash` like products and an optional `[buyer.trigger]` in the shape of a dilemma card's (`content.CardTrigger`, checked through `news.Eligible`, so one trigger vocabulary and one checker); `[buyers]` carries the pacing (`min_gap`/`max_gap`, exactly the dilemma deck's rule), `offer_days`, `blacklist_days`, `respect` for a full delivery, `notoriety_penalty` for a failure and `keep_days`. Every price in the file is a multiplier of the street price in the buyer's city on the day of delivery, never a dollar figure, so a rescale of the economy carries through. The **market sim** deals and resolves them (`market.Sim.deal`, `deliver`, `settle`), and every roll comes off `Tick.Sub("buyers")`, one a day whatever happens, so the deck never moves the home stream: a run with the deck boxed (`cfg.Buyers.Deck = nil`) is the same world as one with it dealt, contracts aside (`TestContractsAreDeterministicAndBoxable`), and the harness leaves the deck **on** in every run, like the rival's offers (an offer nobody answers touches nothing but itself; the buyers' headlines are source `buyers`, which neither notoriety nor the law counts, and pick their template off the same side stream so the news sim's dilemma roll is not moved), which is why no pinned number moved when it landed. One offer is on the table at a time. **State**: `World.Contracts []Contract` (`offered / accepted / delivered / failed / expired / declined`, with `City`, `Units`, `Delivered`, `Premium`, `Street` the price the day it was offered, `Since`, `Expires`, `Due`, `Paid`), `World.Buyers` (`NextID`, `LastOffer`, `Drawn`, `Blacklist`), and the per-day scratch `World.Deliveries` (contract id -> units) the clock clears; zero values are the pre-#71 state, so no schema bump. **Actions**: `AcceptContract(id)` / `DeclineContract(id)` apply at once while the offer is open (`Contract.Open`; `Accept`/`Decline` are the rival's, `Offer` too, hence `OfferContract`), `Deliver(id, units)` queues a handoff against a contract you hold, out of the stash in its city, refused with `ErrElsewhere` unless you stand there (a handoff is what only you can do somewhere; the stock got there by road or was bought there), `ErrLyingLow` on a lie-low day (`SetLieLow` clears the queue: everyone's day off) and `ErrContractDue` past the due day. The market sim hands over `min(queued, owed, stash)` at the city's opening street price times the premium **before** the street orders (a contract has first call on the stash), moves the units and the cash, and emits `ContractDelivered` (`Complete` and `Respect` on the last handoff; `HeatMul`, `Street`, `Signed`); after the price drift it `settle`s: `ContractAccepted` (report-only) for yesterday's acceptances, `ContractFailed` for a contract short at its due day (`TakeCash` collects `PenaltyCash` of the undelivered value, dirty then clean; `Respect` and `Notoriety` on the event; the buyer is blacklisted `blacklist_days`), `ContractExpired` (report-only) for a lapsed offer, and prunes what has been resolved `keep_days`. **Off-corner is the point**: a handoff consumes no `World.Demand`, takes no dial, is not capped by `Heat.SellCap`, needs no corner and pays no lieutenant's cut (it is a `ContractDelivered`, never a `PlayerSold`, so the crew sim never sees it). The **heat sim** consumes it in one block at the end of its event loops: `attempted[city]` exactly as a `PlayerSold` with `Wanted > 0` (#27: a sting on a delivery-only night files pages, one on a night an offer merely lapsed files none, `TestHandoffHeat`) and `heat.Sim.ContractHeat(w, city, product, units, mul)` = `sale_heat x SaleHeatMul x units x HeatMul x product heat / street_units x mul`, SaleHeat's formula less the fill, the corner weight, the dial and the lieutenant, kept beside it and previewed on the market screen; the **law sim** counts the units against `hard_units` in the city delivered in; the **reputation sim** adds `Respect` for a full delivery (the third respect source beside the payroll and a kept deal) and takes the penalty's respect and adds its notoriety on a failure. The **UI**: the market screen's BUYERS block sits under the product table for the shown city with its own cursor (`buyerCursor`, `onBuyers`), reached off the bottom of the table the way the map's routes are and left off its top; while it is live `a` accepts, `x` declines and `d` hands over everything deliverable from that city's stash (on the product table `x` still cancels an order and `d` still turns the launder dial), and the detail under the table is the contract's (pitch, what it pays today against the day it was asked, owed, the heat a handoff draws tonight). The dashboard gets one line (`contractsLine`: contracts held, due today/tomorrow, offers waiting) and an alert for a contract due today or tomorrow, so the screen keeps its reason to be visited; the report's SALES section carries the offer, the acceptance, the handoff and the failure. `harness.Dealer` (`cmd/balance -policy dealer`, in `harness/buyers.go`) is `crewed` that answers the offers where it stands (takes what it can cover from the stash plus one buy, counting the room tonight's street sales make; declines the rest and anything in the other city), buys the shortfall before the restock, keeps a contract's units out of the night's street orders (`reserve`) and hands over as much as keeps the city under the sting line by `DealerMargin` with the night's orders counted, everything on the due day; `harness.Welsher` is the dealer that never delivers its first contract. There is no `-contracts` flag: the deck is on in the harness as it is in the game. `harness/buyers_test.go` pins the acceptance; `TestDealerBeatsCrewed` reads day 120 over five seeds and the tier rows of `TestMoneyCurve` were left alone for #60 to re-measure.

**Supply contracts are the buy side of the routine** (#113, `World.Supply`, `game.SupplyContract`, `market.Sim.Plan`, `ui/dialogs.go`, `harness.Stocked`). The contract keeps nothing back for tomorrow's wages either, as the hand does not: the market sim cannot price the payroll without the crew's tuning. **The receipts**: a contract's buy is a `Purchase{Contract: true, Day}` in `w.Buys`, and the clock keeps the morning's contract receipts through the day (`clock.supplied`: with `Prior` cleared, since the market reset the supplier price after the buy) and drops them the next, so `w.Buys` is nil as before in a run with no contract; `World.Bought` / `Return` are the hand's, `SuppliedIn` / `ReturnSupplied` the contract's (the last receipt first, at the price paid, the supplier price left alone), `SuppliedToday` the morning's units and cost; the news sim skips yesterday's contract receipts in its money loop and counts this morning's, so `CashBefore` holds. **A sell order may count on the morning's contract** (`PlaceSell` allows the stash plus `World.SupplyDue`, the level's shortfall; the UI's and the harness's expected amount is `Sim.Due`, the plan's), because the contract fills before the orders resolve; without it a stocked player's orders run a day behind the stash and it sells half of what the hand sells (measured: $431k against `crewed`'s $580k at day 70; #114's standing orders want the same rule). **UI**: the buy dialog's last step, after the quantity, is `repeat  [once]  keep at` (`dialog.repeat`, `repeatOnce` / `repeatKeep`, drawn by `dialCells` and turned by `stepRepeat`, the helper the sell dialog's standing orders mirror; the sell dialog's quantity max is `sellable` (the stash plus `Sim.Due`) and says `N stashed and M the contract brings in the morning`. **Harness**: `harness.Stocked` (`cmd/balance -policy stocked`) is `crewed` that never buys by hand: each morning it sets a contract for every product the supplier where it stands sells at `harness.StockDays` (1) of the demand the corners it works there serve, cut to the bag's share by demand where the levels together would overfill it (the way `restock` shares the bag), and orders the stash plus what the plan brings; `TestStockedIsWithinTenPercentOfCrewed` pins it at day 70 (median $535k against $580k, 7.8% under: the markup and the routine's bluntness), `TestSupplyIsDeterministicAndSaves` the replay and the save, `TestSupplyKeepsTheLevel` the level every morning with cash and room, what the float leaves short of cash and `Free(city)` short of room, `TestSupplyPlan` the plan's order, and `TestSupplyContractInTheGrammar` the UI. **Standing orders are the sell side of the routine** (#114, `World.Standing`, `market.Sim.standing`, `ui/dialogs.go`, `harness.Routine`).
